# Graphics and software rendering

San Azura's look is stylised low-poly with PBR materials, all from CC0 sources. This page goes family by family (vehicles with the motorbikes and boats, assets, characters, buildings, props, ground, sea), then lighting, the finish pass, weather, and effects and weapons, and ends with the per-tier summary, the measurements and the software fallback. Every family keeps a procedural model as its fallback, so Node tests, `?assets=0` and a failed load still draw the whole game.

## Vehicles

Vehicle bodies have tapered cabins, sculpted flanks, crowned hoods and clearcoat paint. Rounded tyres, open alloy wheels, brake rotors, mirrors, door seams, grilles, bumpers, plates and exhausts give each vehicle more detail. Exterior fittings add one batched mesh per detailed car and follow damage, burning and pool reset. Distant software traffic retains its simplified model.

Cars (`vehicleView.ts`, `vehicleDetails.ts`): every car kind's glass is its own mesh in a dark tint, env-mapped (the sky, or the player car's reflection probe), drawn double-sided in one pass at 0.74 opacity with a Fresnel term that takes it to opaque at grazing angles (no transmission), so cabins read as closed glass with the riders dim behind it; cracked and shattered glass and the fade copies keep the patch. With the asset library loaded, seven kinds are drawn with Quaternius car models (`vehicleModels.ts`): compact `veh.q.suv`, sedan and taxi `veh.q.taxi` (the sedan without the roof sign), sports `veh.q.sports2`, muscle `veh.q.sports`, pickup `veh.q.pickup` and police `veh.q.police`. Each is scaled per axis to its spec box, its length warped so its axles land on the physics', split by material (or atlas cell) into paint, the cruiser's white panels, trim and glass, given a number plate on each end (laid on the model's plate panel where it has one, otherwise on the flattest stretch of the end clear of the lamps, found by casting rays), refined to 0.22 m edges so dents bend it, shaded smooth within a 38° crease and lined inside (dark, inward faces, with bulkheads closing the bonnet and boot) so no look through its glass passes out of the cabin; the view's own materials, interior, seat frame, tyres, lenses (on the model's lamps) and roof gear go on it, and without assets (Node tests, a failed load) every kind is procedural. The Kenney kit is not used: its toy proportions clash with the cast, and its ambulance's cab sits a metre ahead of the sim's front seats. The other kinds stay procedural: bodies fitted to `VEHICLE_SPECS` (within 3 % of length, width and height, wheels on the physics axles), the noses and tails are rounded in plan beyond the arches (`EndRounding`: one shape for the body, glass, fittings and lamps, so the shadow and far versions follow), the wheel arches carry moulded lips (painted on cars, dark trim on the working vehicles), the bus has a raked windscreen, window pillars and kerb-side doors, and the fire engine a raised nose with a chrome grille and aluminium roller-shutter lockers. Paint is a satin metallic base under a glossy clearcoat. Lamps are moulded lens shapes in one instanced batch: unlit by day when off, softly glowing after dark (no glow sprite or light), bright when driven; tyres show a tread, a lighter sidewall and a lettering band. A soft dark pad under every car (one instanced draw, `UNDER_SHADE`, drawn first among the transparent draws) shades the road beneath it, which neither the sun's shadow (none in shade, at night or on Low) nor the screen-space contact shadows draw; with the cast-iron-dark manhole covers (`cityArchitecture.manhole`) this removes the pale disc that showed under cars parked over a cover. The warm-up compiles a whole and a fading view of every kind, so a car's first spawn builds no shader program.
The refinement that lets dents bend an imported body carries the coarse model's corner normals onto its pieces, so a flank shades smooth across each of the model's faces.
(Normals computed on the refined mesh were flat over each face and bent only along its edges, which drew wavy blotches.)
A fittings kit is laid onto each imported body by rays where its paint runs (`modelKit` in `vehicleModels.ts`): a dark grille with satin slats between the head lamps, a dark panel under a satin bar joining the tail lamps, a dark valance across the middle of the tail's foot (its corners turn away and left only scraps), exhaust tips, dark sills between the arches, and a handle at each door's rear edge.
Its pieces follow the body on a 5 by 4 cm grid, a few millimetres proud, and are merged into the trim's one draw; the grille, panels and sills go into the far trim too, 1 cm further out (clear of the depth buffer's precision at 100 m and more).
Each of its vertices is anchored to the refined body triangle under it (`userData.follow`, `FollowSurface` in `vehicleGeometry.ts`), and a dent moves it by that triangle's interpolated displacement, so it stays as far proud of the dented paint at any depth (moved by the dent's own smooth field instead, a fitting on its fine grid sank under the body's 22 cm chords in a deep dent).
A dent turns the normals it reaches by the bend's own gradient (`bendNormal`: n - grad(w) (a . n) / (1 + a . grad(w))), which fades to nothing at its reach, so a dented panel shades as smoothly as the paint round it; flat face normals per moved triangle drew a disc of hard facets with a sharp ring round every dent.
A dented car beyond 40 m draws its own bent body, fittings and livery (the batches' are undented).
Near only, a second pass (`modelDetail`) lays the body's finer form on its paint the same way: shut lines round the boot lid and the bonnet, along each door's edges and over the bumpers (wrapping round the corners to the wheel arches), a housing round every lamp (satin round the head lamps, dark round the tail), drip rails along the roof's edges, a seal along the rear window's foot, round dark lips over the model's polygonal wheel arches, and wipers on the windscreen.
Where a model has no white rear lamp, the made-up reverse lamp stands on the tail's surface under the tail lamps' middle, 10 mm proud (at the lamps' depth it sat 16 cm inside the sedan's tail).
The box vans (van, riot van, ambulance) have back doors (a seam, satin handles and hinges) between tall lamp clusters up their back corners (`tailLamp`).
The van has a sliding door with its track and stiffening ribs across its roof, which the chase camera looks down on; the ambulance has a kerb-side door.
The bus has satin rails framing its window band and a louvred engine grille between tall lamps.

### Vehicle draw structure

A detailed car draws its body, glass and fittings itself (three draws, four with the police stripe); its tyres, rims, lamps, light bar and taxi sign are drawn by `VehiclePartBatches` (`src/render/vehicleBatches.ts`): one instanced mesh per kind for tyres and for rims, and one for every plain box part of every car, which carries each lamp's colour, glow and finish per instance (smashed lamps are dark, charred ones black). The part meshes stay in each `VehicleView` with their transforms and materials (tests and lamp glows read them) but live on `PART_LAYER`, which no camera draws; `GameRenderer.drawVehicleParts()` copies the cars in view into the batches every frame. A car casts its shadow with one merged, shadow-only body per kind (body, fittings, glass and tyres at rest; a wreck has no glass or tyres) on `SHADOW_LAYER`, which the camera only sees during the shadow pass. On every hardware tier, cars other than the player's beyond 40 m (with 2 m of hysteresis) switch to the far version: an undented body joins its kind's instanced body batch (paint as the instance colour), which also casts the shadow, and its glass, fittings and livery join their kind batches, so a far car costs about nothing of its own; a dented one draws its own bent body, fittings and livery. Far bodies, fittings and livery stay out of the contact-shadow pass. Software renderers keep the coarse merged far model at 32 m.

On High at 1920x1080 a sedan 5–20 m away costs about 6.6 calls with its shadow and contact-shadow draws (38 before), and ten far sedans cost at most 2 calls each including the shared batches.

High's drawing buffer is capped at 3.5 million pixels (`HIGH_PIXEL_BUDGET` in `quality.ts`): a 1920x1080 window on a dpr-2 screen draws 2494x1403 instead of 2880x1620; at dpr 1 nothing changes. Contact shadows work at half resolution, draw their depth and normals only out to 110 m (fading out over the last 35 m) and take those draws from the frame's own opaque render list instead of walking the scene again (`shadesContact` picks them: an instanced batch such as a cell of benches, bins, hydrants, lamps or trunks by the bounding sphere round all its instances, so what stands near the camera is shaded wherever it is in the city), and they fade out over 0.25 s and are skipped while the camera moves faster than 8 m/s or turns faster than 1.6 rad/s, fading back in over 0.3 s once it slows. `StaticWorld.groundHeight` looks up decks and ramps in a 16 m grid (identical results to the old scan, about 5x faster). The player car's reflection cube (`src/render/localReflections.ts`, High only) leaves out street furniture with the moving things, but keeps the effects' flash and muzzle lights (`keepLights`): with them hidden, its faces had a different light set from the frame's, and every material in view built a second program on the first capture (about 30, in a frame of up to 3.9 s). The warm-up also filters a blank cube and compiles the player car's reflective finishes, and leaving the car releases the probe without disposing its filter, so the first drive of a session builds at most one program (the part batches' copy of the finished rims).

Motorbikes and boats are procedural, sculpted from lofts, sweeps and lathed parts (`src/render/shapeKit.ts`, `bikeModels.ts`, `hullGeometry.ts`, `boatModels.ts`); the CC0 bikes read as toys and the Kenney boats fit the specs badly, so none is imported. A bike draws its painted bodywork (the cars' paint program, a vertex tone per part), its frame and running gear and its steering parts (fork, bars, mudguard) itself, three draws; its lugged tyres, spoked or alloy rims and lamp lenses go through the part batches, and a coarse proxy on `SHADOW_LAYER` casts its shadow. Every non-paint part samples one shared palette material (a colour texture and a roughness/metalness texture, one texel per finish), so rubber, chrome, cast alloy and leather share a draw. A boat draws its painted topsides (a dinghy its tubes), one palette draw for the rest (vee bottom, cockpit, seats, console and wheel, rails, outboard), the speedboat's windscreen in the cars' glass, and a foam ring laid each frame on the drawn sea round its waterline (two sea samples per ring column; flat at the water elsewhere; absent when sinking or burnt). Its bow and stern lights go through the batches. Measured on High at 1920x1080 (all passes): a sport bike costs 7.8 calls and about 18k triangles, a scooter 7.8 and 12k, a speedboat 7.3 and 13k, a dinghy 6.3 and 13k (d98da7b: 30, 30, 17 and 14 calls). Bikes and boats fade in over `VEHICLE_FADE_IN` (0.5 s) where they appear, their riders with them (`RiderMount.level`), and fade out like cars. The Marina's moored cabin cruisers use the same hull builder (`mooredBoat`), inside the sim's hull and cabin boxes.
Round 9 reworked what reads rough from where the player sees them, keeping every seat, grip, peg, lamp, hull and waterline where it was (`tests/unit/r9_bikes_boats_guns.test.ts`).
The sport bike has a half fairing whose tail tucks under the tank (the full fairing down to 0.43 m read as a bulbous shell with a flat back), a slim upswept tail over a trellis subframe, alloy cooling fins on its cylinder block, a framed radiator, a silencer slung under the tail on the right where the chase camera sees it past the rider's leg, a hugger over the rear tyre, amber indicators, and a plate in a black border with its characters (7,784 triangles before, 8,580 after).
The scooter's steering column, which stood bare in front of the leg shield and read as a black spike, is wrapped in a painted spine down the shield (5,244 to 5,372).
The speedboat has a dark stripe under its sheer and a boot stripe at the waterline (the topsides read as one flat white slab), a white outboard with its dark band and a name board on the transom (5,575 to 5,967); the moored cruiser has the same stripes, side windows and a windscreen laid 6 mm proud of its cabin (a loft of its own poked through it as black shards), and brackets and a ladder under its swim platform (3,147 to 3,447).
Normals of no length at a hull's stem and a deck's bow end, where the grids close to a point, are given their triangle's normal (`soundNormals` in `hullGeometry.ts`): such a normal shades as NaN, which the bloom spreads over the frame.

High's drawing buffer is capped at 3.5 million pixels (`HIGH_PIXEL_BUDGET` in `quality.ts`): a 1920x1080 window on a dpr-2 screen draws 2494x1403 instead of 2880x1620; at dpr 1 nothing changes. Contact shadows work at half resolution, draw their depth and normals only out to 110 m (fading out over the last 35 m) and take those draws from the frame's own opaque render list instead of walking the scene again (`shadesContact` picks them: an instanced batch such as a cell of benches, bins, hydrants, lamps or trunks by the bounding sphere round all its instances, so what stands near the camera is shaded wherever it is in the city), and they fade out over 0.25 s and are skipped while the camera moves faster than 8 m/s or turns faster than 1.6 rad/s, fading back in over 0.3 s once it slows. `StaticWorld.groundHeight` looks up decks and ramps in a 16 m grid (identical results to the old scan, about 5x faster). The player car's reflection cube leaves out street furniture with the moving things.

High adds local ambient occlusion before bloom and six nearby street lamps that light car bodies and facades at night. Lamp selection cross-fades, and broken lamps switch off immediately. Transparent effects and camera-dissolving foliage/characters are excluded from the contact-depth pass so invisible geometry cannot leave dark artifacts; sun and moon shadows still apply normally. Medium and Low keep the same resolution settings as before this visual pass.

### Fades and helicopter crashes

Characters on foot fade in over `PED_FADE_IN` (0.5 s) the first time they are drawn within 200 m, so peds spawning nearby no longer pop in; the player and car occupants are always drawn whole. The fade is a per-instance `instanceFade` attribute on the crowd's instanced parts, applied as an ordered (Bayer) dither in the existing character shaders, and the characters' `customDepthMaterial` applies the same dither so the shadow fades with the body. A character removed while drawn on foot (a cleared body or a despawned ped) keeps its last pose and fades out over `PED_FADE_OUT` (0.6 s). `crowd.firstDrawn` in the browser logs the last 400 first-drawn characters with their position and fade. Vehicles removed within `VEHICLE_FADE_RANGE` (150 m) of the camera and in view (cleared wrecks, despawned traffic) fade out over `VEHICLE_FADE_OUT` (0.6 s): `VehicleView.beginFade()` swaps in `alphaHash` copies of the materials of the meshes the car draws itself and gives its shadow body (and a far car's own dented or coarse body) an `alphaHash` depth material, keeping the version it was drawn in (a far car stays far); `endFade()` restores everything before the view returns to the pool. Its tyres, rims, lamps, roof gear and far body, glass and fittings stay in the shared part batches: every bucket carries a per-instance `instanceFade` with the same Bayer dither as the characters (in its own material copy, and in the far bodies' `customDepthMaterial`), so a fading car adds no draw calls (measured 0 per fading car on Medium and Low, slightly fewer than live on High, where fading bodies leave the contact pass). Warmup compiles the fade copies and the bucket programs, so a fade does not compile new shader programs mid-play. Software renderers pass `alphaHash` and the instance fade through to their simplified materials.

The part batches draw each bucket through a geometry of their own that shares the part's vertex buffers and adds the per-instance attributes. A bucket whose part material or geometry is disposed (the player's local-reflection rim copies, replaced on every finish change) is retired at the next frame's `begin()`, after the frame it was drawn in: its instanced mesh, geometry and material copy are freed, so transient materials do not accumulate. `GameRenderer.hookShadowMap()` wraps three's `shadowMap.render` to show `SHADOW_LAYER` to the shadow pass; three builds a new shadow map when a lost WebGL context is restored, so the hook is installed again on restore (and checked every frame), once per shadow map.

A crashing police helicopter trails fire and smoke every frame while it falls (`effects.heliCrash`). Its descent is tested against the solid colliders and the Ferris wheel's disc (`src/sim/heliCrash.ts`): it comes to rest on top of the first roof it meets (the whole height swept in a step is tested, so the longest 0.1 s frame cannot carry it through a 0.4 m garage slab), including the garages and the police station, with a smaller blast on a garage roof (`HELI_ROOF_BLAST`) so a car parked inside is not destroyed, and it stops against the lighthouse, crane legs and the Ferris wheel instead of falling through them.

## Assets and the asset library

Models, PBR textures and the sky HDRI live under `src/assets/` and are built from CC0 sources by `npm run assets` (`scripts/assets/README.md` describes the pipeline and the output conventions; `LICENSES-ASSETS.md` lists every source). `src/assets/manifest.json` has one entry per file: id, file, kind, byte size, bounds, license id, and for characters a bone map and for vehicles wheel sockets.

`src/render/assets/` loads them:

- **Preload.** `main.ts` waits for the fonts, then calls `assets.preload(onProgress, { backend: browserBackend })` behind the "LOADING SAN AZURA" screen, and builds the game only when it settles. The bar follows the bytes received (each file is streamed; progress is the received bytes over the manifest's byte total, never backwards, at least one update per asset). Requests use the content-hashed URLs Vite emits for `import.meta.glob(..., { query: '?url' })`, which are relative, so the build also works from a sub-path such as GitHub Pages `/gta7/`; `assetsInlineLimit` is 0 so no model becomes a `data:` URI.
- **Timeout and fallback.** Preload resolves, it never rejects or hangs: after `PRELOAD_TIMEOUT_MS` (12 s) it aborts what is still pending and settles. A file that fails (HTTP error, abort, corrupt data) or times out is missing from the library, logs a single `console.warn` (`GTA7 assets: <id> failed (...)`; Chrome adds its own network line), and its view builds the procedural model it always had. A manifest that cannot be read leaves the library empty (one warning). Responses that arrive after the library has settled are dropped, so a model never changes in the middle of play and no shader compiles because of it. `?assets=0` skips the preload.
- **Lookup.** Views never wait: `assets.model(id)`, `assets.clone(id)` (a copy sharing geometry and materials), `assets.texture(id)`, `assets.bounds(id)` and `assets.socket(id, name)` answer synchronously and return `undefined` for anything not loaded. `assets.use(id)` also reports an id the manifest does not know, once. Node tests see an empty library unless they call `loadAssetsFromDisk()` (`src/render/assets/testing.ts`), which loads the committed GLBs through the same GLTFLoader and meshopt decoder with placeholder textures.
- **Warm-up and context loss.** `GameRenderer.warmup()` adds `assets.warmGroup()` (one clone of every loaded model, parked below the map) to the scene while it compiles, so every asset material has its program before first use, on every tier and for the software tier's vertex-lit replacements too; the group is removed again straight after. The warm-up runs again after a tier switch and after a restored WebGL context. Textures keep their `ImageBitmap` (it is never closed), so three uploads them again after a context loss.
- **Debugging.** `window.__gta7.ext.assets` has `report()` (manifest size, loaded, failed ids, timeout flag, bytes, preload and decode milliseconds), `ids(kind?)`, `entry(id)`, `list()` and `warmGroup()`. The warm-up group is cached only once the preload has settled, so models that arrive after an early warm-up are warmed too.

Who uses what: seven car kinds the `veh.q.*` models (Vehicles), the buildings the ambientCG facade sets and Poly Haven's `box_profile_metal_sheet` (Buildings), the ground Poly Haven's `worn_asphalt`, `leafy_grass` and `coast_sand_01` (Ground), the trees the leaf atlas built from ambientCG's LeafSet024 (Props), and the lighting the Kloofendal sky HDRI (Lighting and sky). The people, street furniture, dressing, the motorbikes and boats, the procedural car kinds, the guns and the effects are modelled or generated in code; no asset was imported for them. `LICENSES-ASSETS.md` names every asset id under its source.

## Characters

People are chunky toy figures built in code (`src/render/characterGeometry.ts`): rounded segment parts (torso, hips, head with a face, hair and an optional sweep, caps, sunglasses, neck, deltoids, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, shins and shoes, a collar, an untucked hem, and the police badge and duty belt) hinged on the game's own skeleton (`SKELETON` and the procedural pose in `src/sim/pose.ts`), with the feet planted by the gait (`src/render/gait.ts`). Each character's height, build, skin, shirt, trousers, hair and hat come from its sim appearance. `CrowdView` (`src/render/crowd.ts`) draws every body part as one `InstancedMesh` for the whole crowd, so a crowd costs one draw per part and pass whatever its size.
What is too small to model is painted per pixel (`src/render/characterPaint.ts`): from where a pixel falls on its part and the person's second colour and style (`instanceTrim`, `instanceStyle`, chosen by id in `dressOf`), the shader draws necklines, plackets, buttons, pockets, a jacket's lapels and the shirt under it, belts and buckles, the fly and the stitching of jeans, a cap's panels, cuffs, an officer's patches and the soles of shoes, with no texture, triangle or draw call; lines thinner than a pixel fade to their average.
Nobody further off is drawn cheaper: a person costs 3,548 triangles dressed plainly and 4,552 at most. Fades, the camera's lens dissolve and the foot contact shadows work the same on every tier and on the software renderer; `crowd.drawnHeads()` lists the drawn heads for tests.

## Buildings

Buildings draw with one shared facade program (`src/render/city/facadeShader.ts`): PBR stone, brick, plaster and metal from the WebP sets, an analytic box recess for every window cell with reveals that shade and self-shadow, relief sills, a rough/metal split for the glass, per-building tints, night windows lit per cell inside the recess, and a building fog that caps the haze on buildings so the towers keep their outline in the afternoon. Cornices (0.18 to 0.36 m) and parapets are geometry. Roofs carry HVAC units, water tanks, stair and lift housings, antennas, vents and roof gardens, merged into the cell batches (`rooftops.ts`). Four landmark towers (a stepped crown with a spire, a pyramid, an open crown and a wedge, `landmarks.ts`) are the tallest flat-roofed downtown and midtown towers chosen clear of each other along the sea sight lines. Shops have 178 frontage designs (`storefronts.ts`) with rooms behind the glass, signs, awnings with undersides and planters; at night about two thirds are lit at 22:30, and they close one by one until shortly after 02:00.
Plaster walls (`white_plaster_02`: stucco, houses, hotels and the curtain walls' spandrels) keep the set's fine grain but only `FacadeLook.stain` (0.2 to 0.3) of its broad light and shade: the shader divides the albedo by its mip 4 levels down (about 8 cm on the plaster) and puts that share back, one more texture fetch; at full strength the set's stains, tiled every 2.6 m, read as smudges on every house and apartment.
Every frontage cell with a door has its doorway (`DOOR_OPENING` in `textures/storefront.ts`) drawn 0.14 to 0.6 m behind the front, with reveals, a soffit and a threshold that take the cell's colour just outside the doorway (`frontQuad`); the revolving door's drum stays flush.
Piers of the building's trim (0.32 m wide, 0.1 m out) part the bays of a shop, lobby or loading band and clasp its corners, under a deeper cornice with a lip; parapets up to 45 m carry a coping in the trim's stone.
Hotels run a balcony along every floor of their street walls and 45% of the apartment blocks have one at every other window (`balconies`; of the special buildings only the Hotel Salgado, and on a setback tier none whose slab would meet the lower roof's parapet and coping); two brick blocks in three have a fire escape down a street wall at least three window columns wide, its drop ladder ending at least 2.4 m over the pavement (`fireEscape`); both are in the double-sided street decor.
String courses hang just under their floor lines, so a balcony's slab or a fire escape's landing on that line covers their tops; a landing stands `ESCAPE_FLOOR` (0.3 m) over its line, clear of the ground floor's cornice lip.
The bank's doorway floor is level with its top step (`DoorOpening.floor`, `BANK_STEP_TOP`), and a house's with its porch deck.
Trim under 10 cm (the gables' lap boards, shutters, plinths, copings, downpipes, the attic vents) is about 55,000 of the trim's 236,000 triangles city-wide and casts into the shadow maps with the rest.
As one shadowless mesh (one draw call more) it cost 30,000 to 240,000 more triangles a frame in the street views than it saved, because `CityView.add` splits only shadow casters into culled cells, so the whole batch was drawn in every pass; it stays in the trim.
The door floors take no headlight pool: they are in the storefront material, and the headlights are added to the ground's materials by the renderer.
City houses have lap-boarded gables in the wall's colour round an attic vent (the gable ends were the roof's dark material), barge and fascia boards, a ridge cap, a chimney on three in four, downpipes, a stone plinth with a sloped top (no level cap for the headlights' pools to light) and green shutters at the upper windows the facade paints (`houseShutters`); the county's farmhouses keep their plain roofs.
Each facade wall's per-vertex seed carries its tier's roof height in decimetres in its whole part and the building's night seed in thousandths in its fraction (`facadeSeed`; exact in a float32 below 409 m), so the shader knows where the cornice is.
The layout's heights are no whole number of storeys, and the top row of the window grid ran into the cornice on most buildings (seven brick blocks in ten): a window whose head comes within its style's headroom of the roof (`FACADE_TOP`, 0.35 to 1.2 m) is left out, and the band over the top floor becomes an attic; a house's top window is lowered (keeping 0.3 m over its floor) and shortened under the eaves if it stays 0.75 m tall (`facadeWindowFit`, which the shutters use too).
The upper floors are drawn in the shader, on every tier including Low's flat facades (`uDeco`): brick blocks get a stone lintel with a keystone or a gauged-brick arch over every window (by building), stone sills, a stone sill course under the top floor and a corbel table under the cornice; apartment blocks moulded hoods, a sill course and a painted frieze; offices piers between their bays (a ribbon, every other bay or every bay by building, every bay on the top floor) with reveals in the recess; a glass tier ending over 30 m has louvres in place of its top row of glass.
Downtown's three-tier glass towers draw their podium (10 to 16 m) in the office facade with a pier at every bay; the blue curtain walls have vision glass 2.3 m tall over a spandrel band, the teal ones a 0.3 m fin on every bay (`facadeRelief`).
Office and glass towers of eight storeys or more carry a plant penthouse (concrete round a louvred band, 4 to 5.5 m) clear of the layout's roof props, and three brick and apartment blocks in five raise the middle of their street parapet in two steps; together with the review fixes the buildings come to about 434,000 triangles city-wide (429,000 before), with no mesh or draw call added.
All of it rides in the existing trim, decor, roof and storefront batches, so the city's mesh count and programs are unchanged: the first pass took the buildings' triangles from about 265,000 to about 430,000 city-wide (about 5% more triangles in a street view with its shadows, draw calls the same).
`tests/unit/buildings_detail.test.ts` checks the stain share, the doorways, the normals, the mesh count, the balconies, the fire escapes, the shutters, the roofs in the facade seeds, the windows' fit under the roofs, the podiums and the penthouses.

`CityView.setQuality` sets the facade anisotropy (8x High, 4x Medium, 2x Low, 1 on software renderers) and the window parallax (High and Medium; Low and software renderers draw flat facades), uniforms only. The street decor and the stunt-ramp sides share the decor program (`DECOR_PROGRAM`), so a ramp first seen at night builds no program of its own.

## Time of day

The sky's image-based light (the baked environment map) is re-baked whenever the analytic sky colours change by about 2 % or the sun has fully set or risen, never less than half a real second after the previous bake, and at once after a jump in time (or every quarter game hour otherwise). Between bakes `scene.environmentIntensity` is scaled by the ratio of the sky's current to its baked irradiance (clamped to 0.5–2), so dawn and dusk brighten and darken every frame instead of in steps. This applies to every hardware tier with half-float targets; without them (and on software renderers) the sky light is an analytic hemisphere fill that was already continuous. The player car's local reflection probe recaptures after 0.05 game hours, still at most one capture start per half second. `__gta7.setClockRate(r)` and `__gta7.envStats()` help check this in the browser.

## Finish pass: colour grade and anti-aliasing

Every hardware tier ends its effects with `FinishPass` (`src/render/finishPass.ts`): High runs contact shadows, bloom, finish; Medium bloom, finish; Low finish alone. It works in linear HDR before three's own ACES and sRGB output, so tone mapping and colours stay the same pipeline on every tier. The grade (`gradeAt(hours, weather?)` in `src/render/grade.ts`) is keyframed over the day: the day (9:00 to 16:00) has fuller colour and a firmer curve (saturation 1.12, contrast 1.08), sunlit faces a little warm against shadows a little cool (it was all but neutral, and the city read as a model of itself), golden hour (18:24) is warmer, and night is cooler, slightly desaturated, with a stronger vignette. It applies a luminance-normalised white balance, saturation, contrast in log space around mid grey, a small split tone and a vignette; no parameter moves more than 1e-3 per game minute, midnight included. Cloud lowers saturation and contrast and rain deepens the vignette, from the sim's weather.

High and Medium keep 4x MSAA from the WebGL context. Low uses FXAA inside the same pass: three's `FXAAShader`, patched to detect edges and blend in a reversible tone-mapped proxy (`c / (1 + max(c))`), so edges against bright lamps are not biased toward the light and HDR values are not clamped. The module throws on load if the patch no longer matches three's shader. Software renderers and GPUs without half-float targets have an LDR output where three allows no effects, so they get neither FXAA nor the grade. Tier switches only change the pass's uniforms (no shader rebuild). `__gta7.setPost({ aa, grade, vignette })` overrides it until the next quality change, and `state().render.post` reports `{ aa, grade, vignette }`.

On the M4 Pro at 1920x1080 High the perf playtest stayed at 60 fps in all four segments (final frames 5.3–6.8 ms).

The pass also draws the rush of speed (`FinishPass.rush`, `rushAt(speed)`): in the player's car from 90 km/h, full from 205 km/h, the frame streaks towards the point the road runs to, not at all in the tall oval that holds the car and the street ahead and most at the edges.
It takes ten samples along each streak on High and five on Medium (ten cost the Radeon iGPU 3 fps), started at a per-pixel offset so their spacing shows as grain; there is none on Low, nor with Camera shake off.

What reaches the bloom is capped at `BLOOM_CEIL` (6, scene-linear, hue kept; `capBloom` patches `UnrealBloomPass`'s high-pass shader and throws if three's shader no longer has the line).
The sun's highlight on a car's clearcoat is a few pixels dozens of times brighter than anything else in the frame, and uncapped it bloomed into a white blob that hid the car.

## Chase camera

`chaseOffset` (`src/render/camera.ts`) is where the camera settles behind a vehicle, and `chaseFov` its lens: at rest 3.7 m plus 0.62 of the vehicle's length back and 1.0 m plus 0.58 of its height up, on a lens 10 degrees under the Field of view setting, so a car fills about a seventh of the frame's width (an eleventh on the 68 degree lens it had, from 5.2 m plus 0.72 of the length).
With speed the lens opens by up to 17 degrees while the boom closes in 14% and drops 20%, so the car keeps most of its size as the street stretches past it; the boom used to back away 18% as the lens widened, and its follow point trailed the car by its speed over 18 (2.8 m at 180 km/h), which together shrank the car by a third.
The follow point now aims that much ahead, so it keeps up with the car at any steady speed and still eases out a jolt.
The camera looks at the road 9 m ahead of the car (16 m at full speed), which puts the car in the frame's lower third and the horizon above the middle.
It has weight: it falls up to 0.8 m behind a car that pulls away and closes up to 0.55 m on one that brakes, for a change of pace only (it catches up with a car that keeps pulling), and it leans into a turn by up to 2.6 degrees (not with Camera shake off).

The motion check (`scripts/playtests/motion.mjs`, see the README) measured the car at 141 px of a 1600 px frame at rest and 115 px above 72 km/h before, and 242 px and 222 px now.

Round 8 on 2026-11-13 (`--scenario perf`, 1600x900, the machine otherwise idle): on the RTX 5090 at High every one of the 18 scenes at 60 fps, 205 to 1,009 draw calls, no console errors.
On the Radeon iGPU at Medium this build runs about 1 fps under `a746489`: the twelve scenes that are neither capped at 60 fps nor the one outlier (beach-dusk, 56 against 47) average 43.3 fps against 44.7, all 18 average 48.7 against 49.1, and the driving scenes were 1 to 3 fps lower in each of three runs (single runs, which differ by 2 to 3 fps a scene from one to the next): the new-game street 34 from 36, downtown 40 from 40, the four-star pursuit 41 from 41, the valley pass drive 39 from 41.
That is with Medium taking 80% of the fuller streets' traffic and 70% of their crowd, and five samples in the speed blur; at 90% of both the same three scenes ran at 33, 38 and 38.

## On-foot camera

On foot the camera stands `FOOT_BOOM` (3.2 m) back from the player's head along the view and `FOOT_SHOULDER` (0.42 m) out over his right shoulder, level with his head (`FOOT_LIFT`), on a lens 8 degrees under the Field of view setting (`footFov`, 60 degrees at the default 68).
He stands 47% of the frame's height there, his head just above the middle and left of the crosshair, his soles 8% clear of the bottom edge; it stood 4.3 m back on the full setting, 0.18 m higher, and showed him at 31%.
The far zoom (V) stands 4.6 m back on the full setting, about that old view (29%); aiming stands as it did, 2.4 m back, 0.62 m out and 0.18 m up (`FOOT_LIFT_AIM`) on a lens 16 degrees under the setting, his upper body at the frame's left, clear of the crosshair (level with his head, the crosshair met someone right beside his shoulder at head height and the gun shot him).
With his back to a wall the raised camera (see `RAISE_DEPTH`) opens the lens to the full setting as it rises (`RAISE_WIDE`): 1.2 m from a wall the foot lens held 63% of him in the frame, the setting's 72%.
On a setting under 67 degrees the boom stands further back (`footBoom`, 3.5 m on 62, 4.1 m on the narrowest, 55) so his feet stay in the frame; on the widest, 90, he is 33% of it.
The camera keeps up with him at a steady pace (its follow point aims ahead by its own easing; it trailed a sprinter by 0.34 m) and sprinting opens the lens by up to 4 degrees.
It has a little weight: a change of his pace is a change of the camera's own against his, which a critically damped spring (8.5 rad/s) takes out, so it drops about 0.18 m back as he sets off, drifts up to 0.32 m in as he stops from a sprint and swings a little wide as he turns off his line, and is back in place within half a second; none while aiming or swimming, and no bob or roll.
His pace is taken in the view's frame, so a pan of the view as he walks on (he turns with it) is the player's look and moves nothing; taken in the world, a quick pan at a sprint swung the camera out by its whole 0.32 m.
Leaving a car the follow point starts at his head and the boom eases in from as far back as the chase camera stood, as the aim's does; the follow point used to ease out of the car's body, which put the boom's root inside the car and the camera right over him looking a radian down for the first quarter second.
In the water the camera keeps the geometry it had on land before (4.3 m back, 6.6 m on the far zoom, 0.18 m up, the full setting's lens): it cannot dip under the surface, and the glide over quays and decks is tuned to it; on the closer boom a pan round a swimmer at a deck's edge turned the view 5.7 degrees in a frame.
An interaction marker nearer the lens than the player fades by the boom (`markerFade` in `src/render/markers.ts`, given `CameraController.markerView`): drawn in full while its axis lies within 0.1 m of as deep along the view as he is, gone 1.1 m nearer the lens, so the far zoom, the water and a narrow setting each move the band with their boom; on the 4.3 m boom those were 4.2 and 3.2 m from the lens, which on the 3.2 m boom left a marker a step from him half faded and one beside him at his own distance drawn whole and cut by the frame's edge.
The one he steps straight off fades by the lesser of his distance from it and the camera's from its axis (linear from 2 m down to 1.2 m): the camera reaches its beam as he is 2.1 m out, and multiplied smoothsteps took 0.066 of its opacity in one frame at a sprint.

## Night lights

`src/render/lightSprites.ts` draws the vehicles' night lights on every tier including software renderers. Lamp glows are camera-facing sprites at each working head, tail, brake, reverse and siren lamp (`VehicleView.lampWorldPositions()`), one draw call, so smashed lamps and destroyed cars leave no glow. Each glow's colour times intensity stays below the bloom threshold (2.6), so bloom does not flicker on them; brake glows are about 2.2 times the running tail glow. A second batch, light streaks, draws wet-road reflections (see Rain below). Lights turn on at night or in dark weather: `GameRenderer.weatherDim` follows `sim.weather.state.dim` every frame, and cars light up at `max(night, dim)` (rain and storm, and overcast; not clear or cloudy). `state().render` reports `glows`, `pools` (the cars whose headlights light the ground) and `streaks`.
Headlights light the ground in its own shading, as any light does, not in a decal over it.
`LightSprites` keeps eight lit cars on every tier (`HEADLIGHTS`): the player's own, when its lamps are lit, always, in the first slot and at its full strength, and the seven lit ones nearest the camera for the rest.
Every ground material (roads and their manholes and storm drains, lane paint, tyre marks, pavements, kerbs, lots, lawns, beaches, farm tracks, stunt ramps, bridges and decks: the materials of `WetGround` and `GroundSurfaces`; and the crop fields' slabs, `FieldsView.ground`, which are neither wet nor detailed, so the wheat keeps its ripples) is patched once (`lightGround`) to add, after all its other lights, its albedo times the headlights' irradiance to its direct diffuse light (`BRDF_Lambert( material.diffuseColor ) * POOL_LIGHT * shape * strength`).
So a car's headlights add the same light to a dark county road and to lamp-lit asphalt, the asphalt's grain, cracks and paint show under them as under any light, and wetness, fog and tone mapping treat it like the rest of the ground's light.
The crop fields' ground and plants (`FieldsView.ground` and `FieldsView.crops`: the slabs, and the corn, wheat and sunflower plants, whose world position carries each instance's matrix) take the same light by a rule of their own (`lightGround( materials, true )`): what the headlights add is held under `FIELD_LIGHT_MAX` (`fieldLight`, the shader's maths for tests: a luminance `y` becomes `y / sqrt( 1 + ( y / max )^2 )`, the colour scaled with it, so small light, up to 0.05, is kept within 3 per cent and pale straw's is not ten times the asphalt's), and no spot light shines on them (the player's is the only one within reach: a street lamp's spot reaches 22 m, and the nearest of the 1,149 lamps is 72 m from a field).
Both were needed for a field to read as lit ground at night.
Lit as asphalt is, the pale straw of the wheat slab (`#c9a95a`) took ten times the asphalt's light and read luma 202 to 211 at 7 to 20 m with the plants hidden and the pool alone lighting it, where the county asphalt reads 57 to 78, a flat yellow patch like a lamp's (on Low, with no spot, the wheat read 201 to 214).
And the spot, 160 cd falling with the square of the distance from a point 0.8 m up, lights an upright plant in front of the bumper straight on where it grazes the road: the wheat tufts 3 to 7 m ahead went to luma 235 to 255, past the bloom's threshold, a white blob with a halo (the owner's "glowing sun") that flashed as the tufts crossed the beam.
On a flat field at a steady 12 m/s the lit patch's mean luma moved by 2.2 between frames on average and 12.6 at most (a standard deviation of 5.3 over 5 s); with the spot off it moved by 0.1 and 1.0, with the plants hidden by 0.1 and 0.6, and with the ground's pool off it was unchanged, so the flash was the spot on the plants, not the pool, the car's pitch or the beam's chord (flat ground: the rise is 0 and the pool's strength 1 in every frame).
Now the wheat, plants and all, reads 120 to 138 at 7 to 20 m (154 to 235 before), as bright as lit pavement (130), the sunflowers' soil and plants 83 to 95 (before 94 to 122), lettuce 100 to 110 (121 to 137) and the furrows 52 to 63 (58 to 67), the asphalt's own 57 to 78; no pixel of the wheat reaches 145 (255 before) and the patch's mean luma moves by 0.07 between frames and 0.3 at most, and the wheat's ripples stay (they scale the slab's albedo, which the light takes).

The blocks that stand in for corn and sunflowers past the plants' ring keep the road's rule, spot included.
The patch is a fixed loop over uniform arrays shared by every ground material, so cars coming and going compile nothing and draw nothing; the others fade out over the last 5 m before the eighth's distance (or the 90 m range), so a car joining or leaving them does not pop. The player's car is outside that ranking because its bumper is about 11 m from the chase camera: ranked with the rest, a queue or a jam of nine lit cars about the camera took its beam down to 0.53 and 0.04 of its light.

## Lighting and sky

The baked environment map (the image-based light) blends a reduced, sun-clamped copy of the sky HDRI (`src/assets/env/sky_1k.hdr`, `prepareHdri` in `src/render/environment.ts`) with the analytic time-of-day sky. At every bake the HDRI is recoloured and scaled per channel, with separate gains towards the zenith and at the horizon, so that up-facing and sideways surfaces get the analytic sky's light: the HDRI adds clouds and a horizon to reflections, the clock keeps the colour and brightness, and by night it is as dark as the night sky. Its share is `HDRI_MAX` (0.92) in clear weather and less under cloud; around sunset and dawn its brightness follows the analytic sky smoothed over about an hour (`iblSmoothing`), so the dawn/dusk compensation above still changes the light every frame. Without the HDRI (Node tests, a failed load) the bake is the analytic one, unchanged; software renderers keep the analytic hemisphere fill. `envStats()` reports `source` (`'hdri'`, `'analytic'` or `'fill'`) and `hdriMix`.

At night the sky takes a warm city glow over the horizon (`cityGlow(hours)`, keyed on the clock, 0 by day, with no step larger than 0.5 % of its maximum per 0.01 h), and the cloud deck catches it from below.
Off the city's land the glow gathers over the city: `uCityAway` rises from 0 on the land (its beach and its headlands, Azura Point and Smuggler's Key, included; not the causeway) to 1 at 600 m out, narrowing the glow to the cube of the cosine of the angle away from the city's centre (`uCityGlowDir`), brightening the stars up to 2.5 times and taking 70% of the night glow out of the fog; at 0 every factor is exactly 1, so the city's frames are unchanged.
The finish pass lifts the night black level (`Grade.lift`, `NIGHT_LIFT`) with a cool grey floor that fades out above a few times its size, so dark asphalt reads as a surface while lit facades and the sky keep their brightness, and the hemisphere fill keeps a stronger night floor (`NIGHT_FILL` 0.45).
The golden hour's key light leans golden and the grade towards yellow instead of magenta; noon stays neutral.

Street lamps get a wide haze around their heads at night (`LampHaze` in `src/render/lightSprites.ts`), 1.4 times larger and 1.6 times brighter in rain, sharing the lamp halos' positions and on/off attribute (a knocked-down lamp has neither); it is depth-tested, one draw call, and compiled by the warm-up. Wet-road streaks take the colour of their light: lamps stay warm white, tail lamps red, and the lit bulb of every standing traffic signal near the camera (`StreetLighting.offerSignalStreaks`) adds a red, amber or green streak.

Garage interiors and the foot of walls are patched once into three's lighting chunks (`src/render/interiorLight.ts`, before the first program is built, so no material needs a change of its own and no extra program is compiled). Under a garage's roof the sky's diffuse light is cut to `INTERIOR_SKY` (0.5), the sun's to `INTERIOR_SUN` (0.2) and the sky's reflections to `INTERIOR_REFLECT` (0.85), fading across the open door between 0.6 m outside and 0.8 m inside, so a car driving out returns to daylight within a metre with no step. Next to any building or garage wall the sky's diffuse light loses up to `WALL_AO` (0.42) in the corner, falling off with the distance from the footprint and the height above the kerb over `WALL_AO_REACH` (0.55 m), read from a field of signed distances to the footprints (0.5 m texels, one R8 texture); reflections lose half as much. People get a soft contact shadow under their feet on every tier (`FootShadows` in `src/render/contactShadows.ts`, one instanced draw multiplied into the frame).

## Props, foliage and dressing

Street furniture is modelled procedurally in `src/render/furniture.ts`: one vertex-coloured geometry per kind with its origin at its foot and its parts named in `userData.parts`, fitted within 15% per axis to the bounding box of the simpler d98da7b model and drawn instanced in the same 256 m prop cells. Lamps have a cast base 0.92 m tall in a darker paint (a chamfered foot, a bell with ten flutes and an access door, a moulded ring), a smooth pole tapering from 0.1 to 0.06 m with a collar, an arm with a brace, and a luminaire 0.9 m long that swells from the clamp to its widest over the lens and narrows to its nose, a light skirt round the lens and a photocell, with the lens at `LAMP_HEAD`; 540 triangles drawn (502 before). Benches have seven wooden slats on cast-iron side frames with armrests, bins a ribbed body with a rim and a domed lid, and hydrants a bonnet, an operating nut and three nozzles with caps. Traffic signals have three rimmed lenses under visors on a backplate, at the d98da7b bulb positions. Site signs (`siteView.ts`) have round posts, clamps and a rim round a plate with a printed face and a plain back. Deck rails and bollards are new. The drawn lamp pole and head cast no shadow: a 72-triangle stand-in per cell on `SHADOW_LAYER` does that (the drawn parts have 490 triangles), and `breakProp` and `restoreAll` move it with its lamp.

Broadleaf trees and bushes draw 140 and 72 leaf cards. The cards are alpha-tested (`alphaTest` 0.5, never blended) and textured from `src/assets/textures/leaf_atlas/albedo.webp`.
A fifth of them are the rim's: smaller open sprays near the clusters' surface, their face across the radius, so a crown's outline breaks into sprays from any side; three in five of the rest are dense clumps; and a card's shade runs from 0.62 at the crown's foot to full at its top, so a crown is darker underneath.
Leaf alpha is raised by a quarter for every mip level the atlas is sampled down (`FOLIAGE_COVERAGE`, on every alpha-tested foliage material): a mip level averages a spray with its gaps, and without it a crown 40 m off thinned to a scatter of specks.
The broadleaf trunk (`treeTrunkGeometry`, 242 triangles, 84 before) flares into five root buttresses at its foot (0.3 m out at most, inside the 0.32 m collider), tapers as it bends a little on its way up, and sends four limbs out under the canopy (`TREE_LIMBS`) that fork in two each (`TREE_FORKS`) into the lower clusters; it is barked by a grey-brown albedo and normal map built in arithmetic (`barkTextures` in `textures/misc.ts`, no canvas, so Node tests and every tier draw it), darker at the foot.
Cypresses stand on a short straight trunk of their own (`cypressTrunkGeometry`, 36 triangles) with their column of leaves lowered to about a metre: on the broadleaf trunk its limbs stuck out bare under the column. `scripts/assets/leafAtlas.mjs` builds that atlas from ambientCG's LeafSet024 (CC0) as 2x2 tiles: three leaf sprays with alpha and one dense leaf mass. Each trunk splits into limbs below the canopy. Every card vertex stays inside the `foliageSpheres` envelopes even at full storm sway, so the camera and collision envelopes are unchanged. Palms lean 0.45 to 1.4 m at the top by a hash of their position, through a shear on the instance matrix over a built-in curve, and their trunks have ring geometry, staggered leaf scars in their colour, a bulb at the foot and a boot of fourteen old frond bases under the crown; three dead fronds hang brown beside the trunk under the green ones (their vertex colour is what the fronds' green tint turns a straw brown). The occluder dissolve follows the bent axis in three segments (`occluderOpacityPath` on the CPU, `uOccBend` in the shader).

Pinewood's and the valley's pines (`render/country/pineModels.ts`, prop kind `pine`) are a model of their own: a bare trunk under seven overlapping skirts of needles whose hems are notched like boughs, 96 triangles, one vertex-coloured geometry and one double-sided material with the foliage's wind (the top sways, the foot does not) and near-camera dissolve, tinted per instance.
They are instanced one batch to a half cell (128 m on the hardware tiers): a batch is drawn whole, so the frustum and the sun's shadow take a few hundred pines at a time rather than a cell's thousand, and the county's cull hides the far ones with the cells.
A pine between the camera and the player dissolves as a trunk does (kind `pine` in `OCCLUDER_AXIS`, axis 0.5 to 5 m, `PINE_REACH` 1.3 m subtracted from the sight line's distance because a skirt stands about 2 m off the axis; the CPU `occluderOpacityPath` takes the same reach, so a dissolving batch stays out of the contact-shadow pass), composed with the near-camera dissolve under the program key `gta7-pine`: standing with a pine 1 to 1.6 m behind you, where the boom's trunk logic lets the camera through, no longer hides you.
Of the 2,664 pines in 31 batches at most a few hundred are in view at once (from the lake's shore at dusk the wood's pines are 21 calls and 180 000 triangles of 183 and 0.78 M, shadows included), so no far impostor was needed.

Wind (`src/render/wind.ts`) sways the leaf cards and palm fronds in the vertex shader from render time and world position only, through the shared uniforms `uWindTime`, `uWindAmp` and `uWindDir`; `windOffset` is its CPU mirror for tests. The canopy edge of a tree moves about 5 cm in clear weather and 12 cm in a storm, and nothing below 1 m moves. High and Medium sway; Low and the software tier keep foliage still (`PropsView.setWindEnabled`). Shadows keep the unswayed shape, and the weather changes no program.

The dressing (`src/render/dressing.ts`) is render-only: it adds no colliders, uses hashes rather than the sim's random numbers, and is one instanced mesh per kind and cell. Ocean Beach has umbrellas, towels, loungers, lifeguard towers, volleyball nets and surfboards. Every house lot has yard items (picket fences, mailboxes, shrubs, planters and wheelie bins), Azura Pier has rails and the Marina has bollards. ## Weather

`src/sim/weather.ts` (no three.js or DOM) schedules the weather per world seed: eight three-hour slots a day, each kind picked by a stateless hash of (seed, day, slot) with weights clear 45, cloudy 25, overcast 12, rain 12, storm 6. A storm only comes out of rain or overcast, rain clears through cloud, and day 1 is always clear. Changes ramp over 45 game minutes from the slot's start; ground wetness looks back over the earlier rain and dries over about 1.5 game hours. The schedule never touches `sim.rng`, and a save restores it from the saved day and hour (no save change). `SimOptions.weather` defaults to `'clear'`, so headless sims stay clear; gameplay sims use the Weather setting. Overrides (RAINDANCE, debug hooks) ramp over 20 real seconds by default (6 for the cheat) and are not saved. In storms one strike may fall per quarter game minute (a `lightning` sim event `{ x, z, dist }`, 300–1500 m from the player), deterministic for the seed and time.

`Environment.update` takes the weather on every tier: cloud adds up to 0.6 cover and turns the cloud deck and sky grey (rain and storms darken them; at night the deck takes the city's glow from below), the sun and moon lose up to 80 % of their light, shadows soften to 0.4 intensity, the hemisphere fill rises slightly and greys, fog closes in to 45 % of its clear distance in a storm, and exposure rises up to 10 % under cloud. The env map re-bakes through the existing colour trigger. Lightning adds a two-stroke flash (gone within 0.5 s) to the sky's `uFlash` and the hemisphere light; with Lightning flashes off nothing drawn changes. Hooks: `setWeather(kind | fields, { instant })`, `weather()`, `clearWeather()`, `lightning(x?, z?)`, `forceWeather(mode | null)` (the e2e helpers force clear). On the M4 Pro at 1920x1080 High the perf playtest stayed at 60/60/58/60 fps, and storm at night, in a car, and rain at noon at 60 fps (frames 5.6–6.7 ms).

### Rain

`src/render/rain.ts` draws the rain as one instanced batch of thin streaks in a 48 x 30 x 48 m box around the camera: 6000 drops on High, 3500 on Medium, 1500 on Low and 500 on software renderers, with `uRain` following `weather.rain` (none in clear weather). Every drop's position is a hash wrapped in the box in the vertex shader, which adds the fall, the wind slant and a stretch along the camera's velocity, so the CPU only writes uniforms each frame and the rain keeps surrounding the camera at speed. Drops take `Environment.smokeLight`, so they dim at night. `src/render/rainCover.ts` builds a roof-height map on the CPU (2 m cells from the building colliders, garages and decks such as the pier) into a `DataTexture`; drops under a roof are discarded, so no rain falls indoors, in garages or under the pier.

`src/render/wetGround.ts` patches the shared road, sidewalk and plaza materials in `city/` once (`onBeforeCompile` with a fixed cache key, uniforms only), so weather changes compile no shaders. `uWet` follows `weather.wet`: albedo darkens up to 35 %, roughness drops, and on High and Medium a noise mask thresholded by the wetness forms puddles (fading in over the first eighth of wetness), with ripple rings while it rains. Covered cells stay dry: the puddle mask and its ripples are scaled by the cover-adjusted wetness, so garages, decks and the pier get neither. Low only darkens and lowers roughness; the software path's Lambert stand-ins take the darkening alone. Wetness also flattens 80 % of a surface's normal-map relief (`WET_FLATTEN`: the water fills the pores), so the PBR asphalt turns glossy without glittering under the lamps. The ground dries with `weather.wet` after the rain stops.

On a wet road, the light-streak batch in `lightSprites.ts` draws a reflection under the 64 lights nearest the camera (lamps, head and tail lights together), placed at the mirror point between the light and the camera and scaled by the wetness; dry roads get none. A streak also follows the water's Fresnel reflectance at its mirror point (`waterFresnel`, Schlick's with 0.02 head on): it is at full strength where that reaches 0.2 (`STREAK_FRESNEL`, about 73° off the vertical: a street lamp 30 m away from a camera 2.5 m up) and weaker as the camera looks down more steeply, so lamps down the street keep their streaks while a lamp 14 m away leaves a fifth of one and the lamps beside the camera hardly any; with no such term those near streaks were as bright as the far ones and lay at the player's feet like patches of light. On High and Medium, cars within 60 m faster than 8 m/s on a road wetter than 0.3 throw spray off their rear wheels on the existing smoke layer. Rain is visual only: grip and handling are unchanged. `state().render` reports `rainDrops` and `wet`. `node scripts/playtest.mjs --scenario rainnight --size 1920x1080` runs rain at 23:00 on High through the perf scenario's places (see the measurements below). Wet-road streaks are scaled by how dark it is (`max(night, weather dim)`), so by day in the rain, when the weather's dimming turns car lights on, their reflections stay faint.

## Ground

`src/render/city/surfaces.ts` builds the ground materials. On hardware tiers, roads and car parks use the Poly Haven `worn_asphalt` set, lawns use `leafy_grass` and the beach uses `coast_sand_01` (albedo, normal, and ORM as roughness and AO, from `src/assets/textures/`). Each set is tinted to the d98da7b surface colour. The AO channel darkens only the sky-lit part of the light, so the asphalt keeps little of it (`ASPHALT_AO` 0.1, lawns and sand 0.6): at 0.6 the road matched d98da7b in the sun but lost a fifth of its luma in building shade at noon. Sidewalks, plazas, kerbs and dirt keep their procedural tiles, so the paving joints are unchanged. If a set did not load (Node tests, a failed request) or the renderer is the software tier, the procedural tile is used instead.

A world-space detail patch is chained in front of the wet-ground patch (`onBeforeCompile` with fixed cache keys `…|gta7-ground-<surface>`, uniforms only). It adds:
- Asphalt: macro tone, darker repair rectangles with sealed seams on a 9 m lattice, and thin cracks along a noise iso-line in worn zones, which fade out once they are thinner than a pixel. Road segments also get lane wear from a per-vertex `aGround` attribute (across, from start, to end, half width): polished wheel paths, plus a dark oil strip mid-lane that is heaviest near the junctions.
- Sidewalks: along the kerb of every block's rectangle (and the city's, for the promenade ring) a band of pale granite kerbstones `KERBSTONE_W` (0.3 m) wide with a mortar joint behind it; each 1 m flag of the paving its own tone and warmth, about one in twenty relaid paler and one in fifteen weathered darker; grime gathered along the kerb, on the flags rather than the rain-washed kerbstones.
  A quay's kerb rectangle reaches far past its sides that meet no road (`quayKerb`), so its kerbstones run along the coast road and not at the water or the seams where its strips abut.
  Each of the county roads' mouths through the promenade gets a strip of the sidewalk along both kerbs, its kerb the mouth's rectangle, so their kerbstones match the block's across the road.
  `decodeKerb` mirrors on the CPU how the shader reads the kerb rectangle (`KERB_DECODE_GLSL`), for the tests.
- Tactile pads: at both ends of every zebra that lands on a sidewalk (`tactilePads` in `city/ground.ts`), from just behind the kerbstones to `TACTILE_TO` (0.95 m) out and along the crossing short of its ends, a centimetre over the paving in the sidewalk mesh.
  A pad carries its kerb rectangle reversed (minX > maxX), the shader's flag for buff paving studded with domes 6.5 cm apart that fade to a flat tone once smaller than a pixel.
- Kerb faces and gutters: road dirt, heaviest at the foot of the kerb. A kerb face takes the kerbstones' joints every metre, on the flags' lattice.
- Asphalt by the kerb: grit and dust swept into the 0.6 m beside the gutter, paler and rougher, only inside `uGroundKerbed` (the city's rectangle, set by `buildRoads`): Las Lomas County's roads share the road shader but have no gutter, so their edges stay plain.
- Plazas, forecourts and park paths (`paving`): each 0.75 m paver its own tone, about one in twenty-five weathered, fading out before it would shimmer.
- Lawns: dry and lush patches and clover. Where a lawn meets a sidewalk, its quad reaches `LAWN_OVERHANG` (0.18 m) onto the paving and frays there, with a soil band inside the edge.
- Verges: in front of every house lot (Hillcrest and Westside) a strip of the lawn's grass runs from `VERGE_FROM` (the kerbstones' back, 0.3 m) to `VERGE_TO` (1.8 m) out from the kerb, stopping `CROSSWALK_W` + 1 m short of the block's corners so the crossings land on paving (`vergesOf` in `city/verges.ts`, free of three.js).
  The tyres roar on a verge as on any lawn (`softGroundRects` in `src/audio/audio.ts`). The lamps (0.9 m) and street trees (1.1 m) stand in it; people walk the footpath beyond (`PED_INSET`, 2.2 m). Its edge rectangle puts only the footpath side inside the grass, so that side alone frays and shows soil; the kerb side meets the kerbstones cleanly.
- Sand: colour variation and wind ripples (a normal tilt that fades where it would alias). A wet band runs from the sim's waterline to 8.8–11.4 m inland: darker (62 % less albedo), with roughness lowered to 0.65 (dry sand is about 0.96). The surf's lace, bubbles of 10 to 50 cm, runs up the sand and back with the sea's `surfPhases`, its reach scalloped along the shore. A second, turned albedo sample hides the set's repeat.

Every sidewalk tree whose grate fits behind the kerbstones, outside a verge and clear of every tactile pad (both lie a centimetre over the paving; `gratedTrees` in `city/roads.ts`, 303 of them) stands in a cast-iron grate 1.2 m square in the ironwork mesh: soil round the trunk, a plate with two rings of slots and a paler worn frame, 17 quads (about 10,300 triangles city-wide, drawn with the rest of the ironwork in every view; with the 720 tactile pads, 135 verges and the mouths' kerbstone strips the street detail adds about 12,000 triangles and no draw call).
Manholes (about one per 41 m of road, in lane centres, clear of crosswalks and paint) are added to the ironwork mesh, whose finish is rough and mostly dielectric (roughness 0.85, metalness 0.15), so covers and grates read as dark cast iron in overhead sun instead of a pale sheen; the mesh is wet-patched like the road under it, so the rain darkens it and the headlights light it. Tyre marks (braking marks before the stop lines and turn arcs inside the junction boxes, at about 70 % of signalised junctions) are one transparent, wet-patched `road-decals` mesh for the whole map. Both are placed by hashes of road and node ids (`manholesOf`, `tyreMarksOf` in `city/roads.ts`), never by `sim.rng`. Every ground texture gets anisotropic filtering of min(cap, `getMaxAnisotropy()`), with a cap of 16 on High, 8 on Medium, 4 on Low and 1 on the software tier (`CityView.setAnisotropy`, called from `setQuality`). A change re-uploads the textures once and rebuilds no mesh or program. `__gta7.ext.ground` reports the textures and their anisotropy, a GPU memory estimate (about 18 MB), the manholes and tyre marks, the sim's and the drawn water depth at a point (`seaDepth(x, z)`), and the beach's waterline.
### Las Lomas County

`CountryView` (`src/render/country/`) draws Las Lomas County with the city's ground surfaces and wet patch: grass on the land, sand on its beaches down to the seabed, the causeway's verges, asphalt ribbons along each road's centre line mitred at its bends with lane wear, junction pads, the links' mouths through the west promenade, and the paint (white edges, the highway's dashed lane lines, a dashed yellow centre line that turns double solid round the highway's bends, stop lines at the signals).
The Southgate Bridge's steel through-arch is one merged mesh over the bay bridges' deck and parapets, standing on four portal posts the sim makes solid.
Every mesh of the county is hidden on every tier once the camera is `COUNTRY_FOG_MARGIN` (60 m) past the fog's far end from it (`quality.ts`), where it has long faded into the sky; the fog's own distance already shrinks by night, in haze and on the software tier.
The roads and the paint are a few thousand triangles in all, so like the city's thin ground layers they are one mesh each.
The content passes add their meshes through `CountryView.add`, which cuts a layer into `COUNTRY_CELL` (256 m) cells unless it is told not to (`split: false`).
Before the landscape, on the Radeon 610M (Medium) the county's own scenes drew 73 to 163 calls (the causeway at dusk, the highway at noon, the west promenade at night facing the county), against 517 for the perf scenario's pursuit on the same run.

**Terrain detail by distance.**
The ground is drawn cell by cell, each cell twice (`render/country/ground.ts`, `CountryView.addCell`).
Its near mesh follows the sim's ground on a 2 m lattice wherever a rise reaches the cell, within a centimetre of the cosine slopes, and elsewhere draws the flat land as a few quads and the beaches on a 4 m lattice.
Its far mesh follows the ground on a 16 m lattice, flat land again a few quads, with 3 m skirts hanging under its edges: where it meets a neighbour's finer mesh its chords stray from the ground by under 2.4 m (across Coyote Pass's walls, whose 12 m run the lattice cannot follow), so no gap opens.
A cell whose nearest edge is within `COUNTRY_NEAR` (450 m) of the camera shows its near mesh, farther ones their far mesh: 375 000 triangles of near ground for the whole county (only the cells within 450 m drawn at once) against 9 300 of far ground.
The grass shader turns ground steeper than 26 degrees to layered bare rock (`FS_GRASS` in `city/surfaces.ts`, from the screen-space normal): only Coyote Pass's carved walls, up to 48 degrees, since the ridge's own flanks stay under 20.
The ground takes the terrain fog (`withTerrainFog` in `city/facadeShader.ts`): three's own linear fog on the flat fields and the beaches, so they fade into the haze as the city's ground does and their cells vanish unseen past the cull, and the building fog's cap on the rises from 2 m up.

**The countryside's life.**
Crows, the dairy's dog, the crop-duster, the combine and the dawn balloon (`render/country/life/`) are presentation only: they read the sim's clock, its cars and the player's position and write nothing to it, and draw from no random stream of the sim's.

Past the fog's reach, where the fog has hidden it already, a thing is not drawn, and the crows and the dog are not stepped either; the biplane, the combine and the balloon are pure functions of the clocks and their paths are evaluated every frame, which costs next to nothing.
The biplane's spray and the combine's dust are puffs on the effects' lit smoke layer (`Effects.puff`), so they add no draw calls and dim at dusk; they are let off only within 420 and 300 m of the camera, at the effects' plume rate of the tier (1, 0.7, 0.45 and 0.3).

The skyline itself needs nothing: the camera's far plane is 2400 m, no tier culls the city's buildings, and the building fog's cap keeps the towers.

## Sea

`src/render/waves.ts` (no three.js) defines eight Gerstner waves: a long-crested swell of four waves 44 to 94 m long, travelling within 21° of onshore (towards the east beach), and the wind's chop of four of 4.3 to 33 m on it. Their heights follow `weather.seaState` (0.3 calm, 1 in a full storm): the swell stands at 0.22 + 0.78 times the sea state of its storm height (the open sea's swell is there on a still day), the chop at the sea state to the power 1.5 (`SWELL_BASE`, `CHOP_POWER`), so the open sea's significant height (four standard deviations of the surface) is 1.1 m on a calm day and 2.7 m in a storm, its crests at most 0.85 and 2.3 m over the mean. No two waves within 20° of each other are in a whole-number ratio of length, so their sum does not repeat. Their time phases are wrapped in double precision on the CPU and sent as two `vec4` uniforms, so they never stutter in float32. `src/render/sea.ts` patches the sea's standard material once (on top of the two-layer ripples, uniforms only, one cache key): every hardware tier adds the wave slopes to the ripple normal (`seaWave`, with Gerstner's narrower rise at a crest), tilted more than the waves' own slopes so a metre's swell on a 70 m wave still shows in the light (`NORMAL_GAIN`: the swell's and the breakers' 2.6 times on the grid and 3.4 on the flat plane, the chop's 1.4 and 2.2), each wave fading out as its phase outruns the pixels (`WAVE_PHASE_PX`, 1.2 to 2.4 radians a pixel along the wave's own direction, so a swell crossing the view keeps its bands far further than one running towards the eye), and the ripple UVs are worked out from the world position because the sea mesh follows the camera. The software sea (`softwareSeaMaterial`) uses the two longest swell waves, sheltered, and splits its unlit body (`SOFTWARE_BODY` of daylight) and the folded, lifted baked sky (graded as the dome grades it, and by night in the night sky's blue) by the same Fresnel, with the key light's glint.

Where the waves reach comes from a shelter map baked over the world at start-up (`src/render/seaShelter.ts`, 851 x 494 points 4 m apart, 60 to 100 ms, one RGBA half-float texture of 3.2 MB): per point the shelter (0 to 1), the drawn water's depth and its gradient. Walls are land within 8 m of water deeper than 2.5 m (quays, sea walls, the causeway, headlands' rocks; the beaches shelve too gently to count) and the bridges and the Marina's slips where they stand in water deeper than 1 m (the slips stand for the breakwaters a marina has); with the water cut into the land (the harbour basin, the coves and bays, the canal, and Lake Lomas in Las Lomas Valley) they are the shelter's seeds, and the waves grow back from nothing over 4 to 50 m of distance from the nearest (`SHELTER_RAMP`). Piers on pilings (Azura Pier, the Key's jetty) shelter only where the sea could reach their undersides to within 0.3 m, a storm's tallest crest or 0.4 times the depth (`DECK_MARGIN`), and the waves grow back from them over 2 to 15 m (`DECK_RAMP`): so Azura Pier's seaward half is calm while the surf runs on beside and under its shoreward half, where the water is under 2 m deep. The swell comes from the east, so the shelter is also multiplied by the lee of the land east of each point (`leeOf`, a sweep of each row from the east and running sums down each column, a few milliseconds): its shadow fills in from its edges over a Fresnel penumbra, the root of 72 m (the onshore swell wave, `LEE_WAVE`) times the distance behind the land, softened 12 m either way (`LEE_SOFT`). So the harbour, the canal and the Marina's slips are calm, 5 m off a quay 2 % of the swell is left (a storm's crests reach 5 cm there), Lomas Sound's middle behind the city keeps none of it (only its ends, where it wraps round the city), 100 m behind Smuggler's Key about half, and the open sea and the east beach have it all; the sea west of the county is in its lee. Over the east beach the waves shoal (`seaShoal`): the open sea's waves are capped at 0.4 times the depth (a wave breaks at about 0.8 times the depth from trough to crest) and fade out between 2 and 1.4 m deep into the breakers: the first three swell waves (`SURF_WAVES`), each slowing as the water shallows (in shallow water a wave runs at the root of g times the depth, so over the even 1:34 slope its phase grows as the root of the depth, `SURF_S`: crests some 28 m apart where they break and 10 m at the swash), keeping its wavenumber along the beach (`SURF_KZ`, as refraction does) and in step with its own swell wave where the beach is 1.7 m deep (`surfPhases`, from the beach's start in the layout), summed by their heights (`SURF_W`), so they beat into sets and lulls about a minute apart; 1.3 times the open sea's significant half-height until the depth caps it, steepened (Gerstner's steepness 2.4, its water moving along the depth's gradient); about a metre high in a set where the beach is 1.2 m deep on a calm day. They feel the sand's depth with render-only bars and rip channels along the beach, 40 and 91 m crests that make it up to 30 % shallower or deeper and fade out by 1.6 m deep so the hand-over from the swell stays in step (`surfDepth`, `SURF_BAR`). A set breaks where its height passes 0.8 of the depth it feels, so a set's bigger waves break further out, over a bar first and in a rip late or not at all, and a lull's hardly at all; in shallow water all three run at the same speed, so the set's height rides in with each wave and a broken wave stays broken. The surf's whitewater (`seaSurfFoam`) is a roller up a broken wave's front and foam trailing seaward behind it (`SURF_ROLLER`, `SURF_TRAIL`), brightest just after the break and over the bars, thinner in the rips, with lingering patches between the bores; just before a wave breaks its shoreward face darkens as it rears (`seaSurfFace`, `SURF_FACE`) and a thin lip whitens along its crest; a pixel spanning much of a wave draws the foam's average. Breakers need a gently shelving seabed (`SURF_SHELVE`): Las Lomas County's 1:5 shores and the quays' walls get a wash pulsing at the waterline instead. The swash (`seaSwash`, shared with the sand's lace) runs up the sand 1.4 radians of each breaker's phase after it reaches the waterline, further in a set. Every wave height is under 0.4 times the depth, so the drawn waterline stays the sim's and no trough drains the sand; in a storm the crests stay at least 0.65 m under the decks' undersides and the water two metres off the quays is still.

On High and Medium (`TierSpec.waterGrid`) the sea is a grid around the camera, with the rings `TierSpec.waterRings` names (`SEA_GRIDS` in `waves.ts`). High's fine rings have cells that double as they shrink under a pixel with distance (`SEA_RINGS`: 1 m cells to 80 m, 4 m to 240 m, 8 m to 480 m, 16 m to 800 m; 58,500 vertices and 112,000 triangles in 2 MB of buffers, where one ring of 4 m cells to 800 m took 191,600, 371,000 and 12 MB, mostly in slivers under a pixel tall), so the swell keeps its shape out into the fog, then a flat skirt to 3 km. It moves in whole 16 m steps (`SEA_SNAP`, its largest cell), so every ring's vertices stay on a fixed world lattice and do not swim. Medium's coarse rings are 4 m cells to 64 m and 8 m to 128 m (2,465 vertices, 3,840 triangles), then the flat skirt, moving in 8 m steps: from a low camera the fine rings' cells are under a pixel tall past some 40 m, and an integrated GPU with 4x MSAA shades a pixel once for every triangle its samples touch, so on the Ryzen iGPU (Radeon, 2 compute units) the fine grid ran the whole sea shader three to four times over and its draw cost 1.1 to 3.3 ms in the perf scenes against 0.06 to 0.7 ms for a flat plane (a flat grid of the same cells cost as much as the displaced one: it is the triangles, not the vertex shader, and the wave maths in the fragment shader adds only 0.1 to 0.2 ms on a plane). Medium's sea shader also leaves out what deep water cannot show (`SeaGridSpec.skip`, the `uSeaSkip` uniform: 1 on Medium, 0 on High and Low): deeper than `SURF_REACH` (3.5 m) it sums none of the breakers' terms in `seaWave`, computes none of the surf's phases, roller, lip, swash, face or beach foam, and more than `QUAY_FOAM_REACH` (1.5 m) from a quay wall none of the quay's foam, each exactly zero there (the breakers fade out by 2 m, a set breaks no deeper than that, and the roller, lip and rearing end within 1.7 times it), so the frame is the same pixel for pixel (an e2e test draws Medium's frame with the skip on and off and compares them) while a pixel of open sea costs about a tenth less. Medium's swell is therefore displaced out to 130 m and no further, which from a boat's 4 m height is under 4 pixels of the swell's rise at the edge; past it the swell and the chop still shade (the normals are per pixel on every tier). The vertex shader displaces either grid with the same waves (`seaDisplace`, sheltered and shoaled); each wave fades out before the grid's cells grow past a quarter of its length, between the distances `SeaGridSpec.fadeStart` and `fadeEnd` hold per tier and per wave, passed to the vertex shaders (the sea's, the foam rings' and the wakes') as one uniform (`uSeaFade`) and read by the CPU twin from it (`SeaSurface.fadeStart`, `fadeEnd`): on High the 44 and 57 m swell waves fade by 470 m and the 72 and 94 m ones by 790 m, on Medium all of the swell and the 33 m chop by 124 m, the 17 m chop by 62 m and the 9 and 4 m chop not at all (a 4 m cell cannot sample them), so boats, swimmers, splashes and wakes sit on the surface the tier draws. A ring's rim vertices between the next ring's are placed on its edges (`seaDisplaceEdge`, `aEdge` holding the rim's direction times the next ring's cell), so the rings meet without cracks. Its normal is one up-pointing byte a component (the contact shading's normal pass reads it) and it has no uv. Low draws a flat plane, where the swell shows only in the light as broad bands; switching tier swaps the geometry and the `uDisplace` and `uSeaFade` uniforms only (the grids not in use are freed). The waves are visual only: the sim's sea stays flat at `WATER_Y`, so swimming, floating cars and buoyancy ignore them, and everything drawn on the water is offset to the drawn sea (below). `state().render.waterGrid` reports the grid; `setWeather({ seaState })` pins the sea state.

What touches the water rides the drawn sea through the same CPU twin (`seaHeightAt`, `waveDisplacement` with the shelter map) or the same uniforms. A boat's bow and stern each ride the mean of three points of the sea along the hull, its roll the sea at its sides: in the water each end is pulled to its sea by a damped spring (`HULL_SPRING`, `HULL_DAMP`, buoyancy and planing lift), stiff enough to ride the swell within a few centimetres and soft enough to plough through the short chop at speed, and out of it falls freely (`HULL_FALL`). So a speedboat run fast into rough water leaves the crests and slams down beyond them, its bow digging into a face it cannot rise to (on a storm at 25 m/s, up to 0.36 m clear, 0.14 m into it and eight slams in five seconds), throwing spray at its bow and shaking the camera riding it; on a calm day the hull heaves about a metre with the swell and rides rather than flies. (Following the sea up at once, as a first version did, handed the hull the full rise of every face it met and threw it metres clear in a storm.) A swimmer is drawn riding the surface, his gun held up with him and its flash and tracers leaving from where it is drawn. A boat publishes the move from the sim's flat pose of its hull to the one it draws on the swell (`RiderMount.simToDrawn`, applied by `onDrawn`), so a rider's drive-by gun stays at his window on the heaving hull (it hung up to 0.8 m off it in a storm), and its muzzle flash and tracers, and a burning boat's smoke, leave from there. The camera stays 0.35 m over the drawn sea under it (the surface is invisible from below), and rises and falls with a swimmer and with a boat afloat, eased in both (so it rides the swell but not every bump of a storm's chop). Foam rings, boat wakes and the foam ring round a hull, splashes, a round's splash and tracer end, a blast's water plume, a car's wading spray, the Gull Regatta's buoys and the objective markers over the water all go on the drawn surface; the Marina's moored boats are props at the flat sea's level, which the Marina's shelter keeps true.

The sea is lit as water rather than as a blue surface. Its body is the light scattered back up out of the water, dark and greyed (`SEA_MID` `#0e4656` by the quays, `#0a2c40` past about 9 m, `#061a29` offshore), and its F0 is water's 0.02 with no metalness, so three's lighting splits each pixel by Fresnel between that body under the camera and the sky towards the horizon. The reflected sky folds back above the horizon (a ripple turned away from the eye reflects the next wave's face, not the environment map's dark under-horizon haze, which drew dark blotches on grazing water), is lifted to the sky dome's own brightness by day (the map holds three quarters of it for the scene's lighting, so grazing water meets the horizon without a seam), is regraded from the horizon up as the dome grades it (the map turns from the horizon colour to the top colour by `smoothstep(0, 0.6, h)`, the dome by `pow(h, 0.55)`, so 6° up the 16:00 map is still the warm horizon's beige, which the water 20 to 150 m out mirrored as tan mud at the fraction of its brightness where ACES keeps its colour; the no-map path and the software sea grade their baked sky by `pow(h, 0.55)` too), and by night takes the night sky's moonlit blue at its own luminance (`SEA_NIGHT_SKY`: at 20:30 the map's palette still holds the sunset's red, which turned the night sea mauve) with a share of the dome's night glows, which the map lacks (`SEA_SKY_GLSL`): the glow all round the horizon faintly, and the city's light only looking towards the city, broken by bearing into lights (`CITY_STREAKS`) that the ripples draw out into warm streaks under the skyline (`SEA_NIGHT_GLOW`). The map has no moon either, so the sea adds the dome's moon halo, five times as strong and stretched in elevation (`SEA_MOON_PATH`): at grazing angles the waves spread a reflection up and down far more than sideways, so it runs from the moon's glints to the horizon as a soft path. Most of the map's broad sun glow is taken back out of it (`SKY_SUN_GLOW.taken`, 0.7: the map holds a little less of the glow than its formula gives, so taking all of it clamped the reflection round the glitter to black), the subtracted lobe widened by the lookup's blur (2 alpha squared), and the dome's own, dimmer glow round the sun is mirrored instead (`SKY_SUN_HALO`), so the water round the sun's glitter is lit as the sky round the sun is; the regrade above fades out between about 17 and 30 degrees up, where the map has reached its top colour while the dome still holds some of the horizon's and the ratio of the top's near-black red would turn that glow salmon. The key light glints off the ripples, through the sea's own direct light (`SEA_DIRECT_GLSL`), a Beckmann lobe (the Gaussian slopes of a wind-blown sea, without GGX's long tails, which spread a low sun's hundredfold-bright glint across the whole sea) capped at `SEA_GLINT_MAX` so bloom does not turn the path into a solid column; the key light is the moon by night, so the moon glitters too. Five ripple layers (the two of the environment's `twoLayerRipples`, `RIPPLE_C`, `RIPPLE_D`, and the 0.87 m capillary layer `RIPPLE_E`) each fade out as a pixel's footprint on the water grows from 4 % to 25 % of their tile (`RIPPLE_FADE`), and the mean square slope they lose, with that of the waves faded on the pixels (`seaWave`'s lost), is added to the roughness (Beckmann's alpha squared), so the water is as smooth as three allows near the camera (`SEA_ROUGHNESS` 0.06, where the ripples glint) and calms with distance into a sheen whose sun path widens rather than vanishing. The faded slopes blur the sky the far sea mirrors but do not dim it: three's reflection weight is read again at `SEA_ROUGHNESS` (at the faded roughness of 0.24 to 0.29 its masking halved the grazing reflection, while near the camera the same slopes, drawn as normals, reflect unmasked). The chop is steeper than before (normal scale 0.36) and roughens with the sea state. A crest's height over the most the waves can add up to (`seaWave`'s height) makes light glow green-blue through the crests for an eye looking towards the light (`CREST_GLOW`), steep crests more; whitecaps break on the crests whose compression, Gerstner's sum of steepness times slope (how near they are to breaking), passes 0.2 and cover them by 0.45 (`WHITECAP_STEEP`): patches from the foam noise with holes in their lace, softening to a faint even cover, as thick as the sea's tallest crests break, where a pixel outgrows them. The threshold sees only the waves the pixel can show (each wave's steepness fades as its slope does) and widens by its input's change across the pixel and by 1.5 standard deviations of the steepness it cannot show (`WHITECAP_SPREAD`), so a storm's whitecaps keep soft edges and do not shimmer into rows of specks on the swell's far faces; the lace's holes fade out as a pixel nears their size, and no cover is allowed where even every crest meeting stays under 0.18 (`WHITECAP_MOST`: never on a calm day). A calm day's and plain rain's crests reach at most 0.16 (none); a storm's 0.69, and they start breaking from about sea state 0.55.

Boats under way leave a wake (`src/render/boatWakes.ts`): a stern point every 1.6 m, and ribbons over them for the churned trail (white foam over pale turquoise aerated water) and the Kelvin wake's two arms, which run out from the hull's sides at 19.5° to the track and widen as they go, laid on the drawn waves. The foam stays put on the water and breaks into patches as it fades, the trail first and the arms by 10 s. Eight boats of forty points share one fixed pool and one draw call, rewritten on the CPU each frame without allocating, on the tiers that draw boat spray.

The shoreline is analytic, from the layout bounds and `BEACH_W`, `SAND_DROP`, `CURB_H`, `SEABED_Y`: the shader works out the water depth over the beach's sand slope and its drop-off (blending into deep water off the beach's ends), tints the shallows turquoise over the first 3 m of depth (and toward wet sand at the waterline), draws the surf's whitewater (above), and a noisy, gently pulsing foam strip 0.5 to 1.5 m wide along the west, north and south quay walls. Foam is part of the albedo, so it darkens at night. The software sea gets the shallows and the surf's whitewater.
The sea's colour follows a drawn seabed that keeps falling offshore. Past 25 m outside the land rectangle it drops another 0.11 m per m (`seaShelfDepth`/`seaDrawnDepth`, render only; the sim's `waterDepth` stops at 7 m). Turquoise shallows (`#2fa89e`) give way to the material's dark teal by 3 m, then darken steadily through the open-water body (`#0a2c40`, reached by 9 m: the quays' 7 m water is most of the way there) to the darker offshore one (`#061a29`) by 30 m, the two ramps overlapping so the colour never pauses. The sand tint at the waterline is now a wet teal (`#86a89a`) and fades out within 0.15 m of depth. The ripple tile's UV is warped, and its strength scaled, by low-frequency samples of the tile itself (gently: warps about twice as strong fold the tile into streaks that fan out across the foreground). A third, finer layer (2.43 m tile, scale ratio 2.74, turned and drifting its own way) and a fourth (the first layer's tile shrunk 1.66 times and turned 53° the other way, `RIPPLE_D`) take over part of the ripple power from the first two (`RIPPLE_AB_GAIN` 0.8), so the repeats never line up and the first layer's longest trains no longer form a regular pattern: in 20 m top-down views at noon the autocorrelation's highest secondary peak, after a 33-pixel high-pass that removes the sky gradient and the sun's glint, fell from 0.25–0.35 (d98da7b) to 0.06–0.15. The surf's whitewater dissolves 4.1 to 7.6 m out (`SURF_BAND_FADE`, 0.12 to 0.22 m deep), short of the swash, so the two never merge into a sheet: the foam at the waterline is the swash, which surges 1.3 to 2.5 m up and draws back with the breakers, and the sand's lace, centred within about a metre of the sim's waterline. Foam rings the pilings of every deck (`setPilings`, an analytic copy of the lattice in `city/coast.ts`, up to 0.7 m from each piling's side); far off the ring widens to at least 2.5 screen pixels (`PILING_FOAM_PX`, up to 1.1 m), so it does not thin to nothing at 30 m. Foam's indirect light is raised by `FOAM_SKY_LIFT` (1.6 times its share), so a ring in a deck's shadow stays white against the sky the water reflects, and at night it only scales the dim sky light.

The sea knows Las Lomas County's beaches through a 1024 x 512 half-float depth map over the county's box (`countyShore` in `src/render/sea.ts`, x -2345 to -655 and z -625 to 625, 1.65 by 2.4 m a texel and 1 MB, baked at start-up in about 40 ms from the county's ground; the first county's 512 x 512 over its first box was 1.9 by 2.4 m, and over the valley's wider one it would have been 3.3 by 2.4, so the map doubles along x to keep the shallows as sharp): `seaDepthAt` takes the shallower of the city's analytic depth and the map, one texture fetch per sea fragment, so the shallows, the surf bands and the swash follow the county's coves and points (the waves' shoaling takes the same depth from the shelter map); the shelf falls away from the nearer of the city's land and the county's (the shader loops over exactly the 14 rectangles of `Country.shelf`, the county's land with Lake Lomas filled in, since a lake has no shelf to fall away along), and the quay foam stays the city's.
Inside the box round Lake Lomas's water (`CountyShore.lake`, the `uLakeBox` uniform) the same shader draws fresh water: `0x4f7d57` shallows over a muddy `0x6b6a47` bed through the thinnest water, a darker `0x143f3c` body, and 85 % of the surf bands and the swash taken off (`seaInLake`), so the lake has no white line along its shore; the CPU twins `seaShallowsCpu` and `seaOnBeach` follow.
The CPU twins and the software sea take the same depth.

The renderer ends a bullet's tracer where its path crosses the surface over water and draws a small splash there, on the drawn swell, instead of the seabed impact (render only). A rocket that crosses the surface over water before it hits anything goes off at the surface (`combat.ts`) with a big `splash` event; a blast within 0.3 m of the sim's surface over water is drawn as a water plume on the drawn one (a white column, a spray ring, mist and a wide foam ring, with no fire, smoke, sparks or flash light). Splashes throw a crown of droplets that streak along their flight, a mist puff and a foam ring (`src/render/foamRings.ts`, one instanced draw) that spreads on the sea and rides its waves through the shared uniforms; a bullet's ring spreads to 3 m across over 1.5 s. On the M4 Pro at 1920x1080 High the perf playtest measured 60/60/55/60 fps and `rainnight` 60/60.

## Effects and weapons

Guns are modelled (`src/render/weaponModels.ts`): slide or receiver, raked grip, trigger guard, sights, a hollow muzzle and, per gun, a magazine, pump, handguard, stock, compact sight or scope, built from parts in the weapon's own frame and merged with vertex colours into one geometry per gun (510 to 600 triangles: pistol 510, SMG 580, shotgun 568, rifle 600, launcher 548, sniper 594; 438 to 858 before round 9).
The bodies are side profiles extruded across the gun with their edges chamfered and their outlines exact (`slab`, the bevel cut inward), so the sockets read off their bounds stay put; ports, serrations and slots are flat panels on them.
From the aiming camera a pistol is about 25 px long, so the tones carry it: a grey steel slide or receiver over black polymer, bare steel at the crown, wood, olive or tan furniture, white sight dots; under the crowd's one gun material (metalness 0.45) anything darker than about 0x303030 reads as black, which every part of the guns before round 9 did.
Their sockets are read off the parts (muzzle front face, grip centre, butt or tube centre) and sit on `WEAPON_POINTS` (sim/pose.ts) to under 0.5 cm, so the sim's shot origins, the drawn muzzle and the tracer start agree, and the forend's underside lies 0 to 4 cm over the off hand's point (`tests/unit/r9_bikes_boats_guns.test.ts`).
The crowd draws them as before: one InstancedMesh per gun kind for everyone holding it, placed by the sim's `WeaponPose`.
The bat, knife, grenade and molotov are modelled in the same file along +y out of the fist (`heldModel`: a lathed bat with its knob and taped handle, a knife with a guard, pommel and pointed blade, a ribbed grenade with its spoon and ring, a bottle with its rag; bat 160, knife 128, grenade 378, molotov 212 triangles), held coloured by their vertices and thrown as the same models.
A weapon pickup is that weapon's own model (`pickupModel`), centred and grown to 0.7 m where it is shorter, so a pistol lying 20 m off still reads; before, every weapon pickup was one box gun.
Every weapon geometry drops its triangles of no area and gives any vertex without a unit normal its face's.

Explosions (`src/render/effects.ts`, `src/render/fx/`) are a fireball of volume puffs whose sizes, heat and opacity are pure functions of age and a presentation seed (`fx/fireball.ts`, never `sim.rng`): it reaches full size within about 0.35 s, burns yellow-white to orange, cools into dark smoke and is gone by 1.85 s, scaled to the blast radius (car 7.5 m, rocket 8 m, grenade 6 m, helicopter 4 to 9 m). Spark streaks (life at most 0.6 s), a dust ring and the first dark smoke come with it, the flash light lights the road as before, and a blast near the ground leaves a scorch. Puffs (`fx/puffs.ts`) are camera-facing quads shaded with a tileable noise texture into lumpy, top-lit volumes, with an HDR temperature ramp for fire and the scene light for smoke; premultiplied alpha lets hot cores glow while cool smoke occludes, and every fireball, flame and smoke puff is one instanced draw, sorted back to front. They fade where they meet the ground and when the eye is inside them. A burning wreck gives flame tongues and a dense dark plume rising past 8 m within 3 s and widening with height; it thins over the burn's last 10 s and stops when the sim's burn ends (25 s). Molotov fires give flames and a smaller plume, never a fireball, and scorch the ground under them.

Vehicle explosions throw modelled debris (`fx/debris.ts`: bent panels, bumpers, burnt wheels, struts, mirrors, crumpled chunks; crash parts use the same pieces). All shapes are in one geometry and a per-instance shape index collapses the others in the vertex shader, so any mix of pieces is one draw and one shadow draw. Pieces bounce off walls (the sim's line of sight), settle flat with their lowest vertex on the highest ground under them and shrink away after 12 s. Scorches (`fx/scorch.ts`) are 17 x 17 grids draped on `groundHeight`, a centimetre up and depth-offset; points over water or beside a height step drop out, so nothing is drawn on the sea or across a kerb's face. They multiply what is under them (wet ground, night light), last 26 s and fade over 4 s, with a cap where the oldest starts fading as the cap is reached. Wrecks of cars, bikes and boats share one burnt material (`fx/burnt.ts`): soot and char with flaking bare metal and a little rust from 3D noise of the model-space position, on the near body, its dents and the far batches.

Counters and caps are in `state().render.effects` (live, peak and cap per type) and `__gta7.ext.fx` (`stats`, `lobes`, `debris`, `scorches`, `scorchAt`). Pools are built for High once; a tier switch only lowers or raises the caps, and the warm-up compiles the puff, decal and debris programs, the debris shadow program and the burnt material's plain, instanced and fade variants.

## Software fallback

Hardware rendering keeps the existing physical materials, procedural sky, reflections, shadows,
bloom, and High/Medium/Low resolution settings. Static city surfaces and street furniture are
batched in 256 m cells (thin ground layers in one mesh each) so geometry outside the camera can be
skipped; software renderers keep 128 m cells.

Software rasterisers (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, and similar renderers) select a separate path at boot:

- LDR output with the same ACES tone mapping and sRGB output conversion.
- Diffuse lighting evaluated at vertices, retaining textures, animated window occupancy,
  emissive lamps, character fading, and foliage fading.
- A simpler animated sky and ocean, with atmospheric visibility ending at 220 m.
- One draw per city material. Visible cell indices are compacted without moving vertex data.
- Nearby street furniture, spatial signal and roof-light batches, and a single-draw distant
  vehicle model beyond 32 m. The player's car retains its articulated, deformable model.
- No shadow maps, bloom, MSAA, or dynamic point/spot lighting on a software renderer.

Software Low renders at up to 0.45 of CSS resolution with a 130,000 pixel drawing-buffer budget;
Medium uses 0.5 / 200,000 pixels, and High uses 0.67 / 350,000 pixels. The budget prevents a larger
window or high-DPI screen from multiplying the software rasterisation cost. Menus, HUD text,
navigation, and the minimap retain their CSS resolution. Selecting High increases world resolution
on software GPUs; it does not enable the physical hardware rendering path.

Skid marks submit only written segments and stop drawing after fading. Broken prop bounds are
recomputed, and lamp clip attributes and traffic-light indexing survive spatial batching.

## Earlier software measurements

Chrome with SwiftShader, a 960 × 540 viewport, Low settings, and a stationary new-game street view
on the development host measured these actual animation-frame rates (elapsed wall time, not the
simulation's clamped timestep):

| Rendering configuration | Actual FPS |
| --- | ---: |
| Original build, 0.67 scale | 2.08 |
| Optimised geometry/shaders, 0.67 scale | 7.5–8.6 |
| Optimised geometry/shaders, 0.50 scale | 11.65 |
| Optimised geometry/shaders, 0.45 scale | 14.65 |

The original scene submitted about 481,000 triangles and 418 draws. The optimised scene submits
roughly 56,000–70,000 triangles and 106–129 draws, depending on ambient traffic. The live world
continues to evolve during these samples, so these are representative measurements rather than
deterministic benchmark scores. Hardware GPU performance has not been measured on this host.

At 0.45 scale the sampled frame interval was 66.7 ms while JavaScript work was about 6.1 ms;
software rasterisation and presentation remain the limiting cost. Hardware acceleration is still
preferable for smooth driving and the full visual quality.
