# Graphics and software rendering

San Azura's look is stylised low-poly with PBR materials, all from CC0 sources. This page goes family by family (vehicles with the motorbikes and boats, assets, characters, buildings, props, ground, sea), then lighting, the finish pass, weather, and effects and weapons, and ends with the per-tier summary, the measurements and the software fallback. Every family keeps a procedural model as its fallback, so Node tests, `?assets=0` and a failed load still draw the whole game.

## Vehicles

Vehicle bodies have tapered cabins, sculpted flanks, crowned hoods and clearcoat paint. Rounded tyres, open alloy wheels, brake rotors, mirrors, door seams, grilles, bumpers, plates and exhausts give each vehicle more detail. Exterior fittings add one batched mesh per detailed car and follow damage, burning and pool reset. Distant software traffic retains its simplified model.

Cars (`vehicleView.ts`, `vehicleDetails.ts`): every car kind's glass is its own mesh in a dark tint, env-mapped (the sky, or the player car's reflection probe), drawn double-sided in one pass at 0.74 opacity with a Fresnel term that takes it to opaque at grazing angles (no transmission), so cabins read as closed glass with the riders dim behind it; cracked and shattered glass and the fade copies keep the patch. With the asset library loaded, seven kinds are drawn with Quaternius car models (`vehicleModels.ts`): compact `veh.q.suv`, sedan and taxi `veh.q.taxi` (the sedan without the roof sign), sports `veh.q.sports2`, muscle `veh.q.sports`, pickup `veh.q.pickup` and police `veh.q.police`. Each is scaled per axis to its spec box, its length warped so its axles land on the physics', split by material (or atlas cell) into paint, the cruiser's white panels, trim and glass, given a number plate on each end (laid on the model's plate panel where it has one, otherwise on the flattest stretch of the end clear of the lamps, found by casting rays), refined to 0.22 m edges so dents bend it, shaded smooth within a 38° crease and lined inside (dark, inward faces, with bulkheads closing the bonnet and boot) so no look through its glass passes out of the cabin; the view's own materials, interior, seat frame, tyres, lenses (on the model's lamps) and roof gear go on it, and without assets (Node tests, a failed load) every kind is procedural. The Kenney kit is not used: its toy proportions clash with the cast, and its ambulance's cab sits a metre ahead of the sim's front seats. The other kinds stay procedural: bodies fitted to `VEHICLE_SPECS` (within 3 % of length, width and height, wheels on the physics axles), the noses and tails are rounded in plan beyond the arches (`EndRounding`: one shape for the body, glass, fittings and lamps, so the shadow and far versions follow), the wheel arches carry moulded lips (painted on cars, dark trim on the working vehicles), the bus has a raked windscreen, window pillars and kerb-side doors, and the fire engine a raised nose with a chrome grille and aluminium roller-shutter lockers. Paint is a satin metallic base under a glossy clearcoat. Lamps are moulded lens shapes in one instanced batch: unlit by day when off, softly glowing after dark (no glow sprite or light), bright when driven; tyres show a tread, a lighter sidewall and a lettering band. A soft dark pad under every car (one instanced draw, `UNDER_SHADE`, drawn first among the transparent draws) shades the road beneath it, which neither the sun's shadow (none in shade, at night or on Low) nor the screen-space contact shadows draw; with the cast-iron-dark manhole covers (`cityArchitecture.manhole`) this removes the pale disc that showed under cars parked over a cover. The warm-up compiles a whole and a fading view of every kind, so a car's first spawn builds no shader program.
The refinement that lets dents bend an imported body carries the coarse model's corner normals onto its pieces, so a flank shades smooth across each of the model's faces.
(Normals computed on the refined mesh were flat over each face and bent only along its edges, which drew wavy blotches.)
A fittings kit is laid onto each imported body by rays where its paint runs (`modelKit` in `vehicleModels.ts`): a dark grille with satin slats between the head lamps, a dark panel under a satin bar joining the tail lamps, a dark valance across the middle of the tail's foot (its corners turn away and left only scraps), exhaust tips, dark sills between the arches, and a handle at each door's rear edge.
Its pieces follow the body on a 5 by 4 cm grid, a few millimetres proud, and are merged into the trim's one draw; the grille, panels and sills go into the far trim too, 1 cm further out (clear of the depth buffer's precision at 100 m and more).
Each of its vertices is anchored to the refined body triangle under it (`userData.follow`, `FollowSurface` in `vehicleGeometry.ts`), and a dent moves it by that triangle's interpolated displacement, so it stays as far proud of the dented paint at any depth (moved by the dent's own smooth field instead, a fitting on its fine grid sank under the body's 22 cm chords in a deep dent).
A dent turns the normals it reaches by the bend's own gradient (`bendNormal`: n - grad(w) (a . n) / (1 + a . grad(w))), which fades to nothing at its reach, so a dented panel shades as smoothly as the paint round it; flat face normals per moved triangle drew a disc of hard facets with a sharp ring round every dent.
A dented car beyond 40 m draws its own bent body, fittings and livery (the batches' are undented).
Near only, a second pass (`modelDetail`) lays the body's finer form on its paint the same way: shut lines round the boot lid and the bonnet, along each door's edges and over the bumpers (wrapping round the corners to the wheel arches), a housing round every lamp (satin round the head lamps, dark round the tail), drip rails along the roof's edges, a seal along the rear window's foot, round dark lips over the model's polygonal wheel arches, and wipers on the windscreen.
Where a model has no white rear lamp, the made-up reverse lamp stands on the tail's surface under the tail lamps' middle, 10 mm proud (at the lamps' depth it sat 16 cm inside the sedan's tail).
The box vans (van, riot van, ambulance) have back doors (a seam, satin handles and hinges) between tall lamp clusters up their back corners (`tailLamp`).
The van has a sliding door with its track and stiffening ribs across its roof, which the chase camera looks down on; the ambulance has a kerb-side door.
The bus has satin rails framing its window band and a louvred engine grille between tall lamps.

### Vehicle draw structure

A detailed car draws its body, glass and fittings itself (three draws, four with the police stripe); its tyres, rims, lamps, light bar and taxi sign are drawn by `VehiclePartBatches` (`src/render/vehicleBatches.ts`): one instanced mesh per kind for tyres and for rims, and one for every plain box part of every car, which carries each lamp's colour, glow and finish per instance (smashed lamps are dark, charred ones black). The part meshes stay in each `VehicleView` with their transforms and materials (tests and lamp glows read them) but live on `PART_LAYER`, which no camera draws; `GameRenderer.drawVehicleParts()` copies the cars in view into the batches every frame. A car casts its shadow with one merged, shadow-only body per kind (body, fittings, glass and tyres at rest; a wreck has no glass or tyres) on `SHADOW_LAYER`, which the camera only sees during the shadow pass. On every hardware tier, cars other than the player's beyond 40 m (with 2 m of hysteresis) switch to the far version: an undented body joins its kind's instanced body batch (paint as the instance colour), which also casts the shadow, and its glass, fittings and livery join their kind batches, so a far car costs about nothing of its own; a dented one draws its own bent body, fittings and livery. Far bodies, fittings and livery stay out of the contact-shadow pass. Software renderers keep the coarse merged far model at 32 m.

On High at 1920x1080 a sedan 5–20 m away costs about 6.6 calls with its shadow and contact-shadow draws (38 before), and ten far sedans cost at most 2 calls each including the shared batches.

High's drawing buffer is capped at 3.5 million pixels (`HIGH_PIXEL_BUDGET` in `quality.ts`): a 1920x1080 window on a dpr-2 screen draws 2494x1403 instead of 2880x1620; at dpr 1 nothing changes. Contact shadows work at half resolution, draw their depth and normals only out to 110 m (fading out over the last 35 m) and take those draws from the frame's own opaque render list instead of walking the scene again (`shadesContact` picks them: an instanced batch such as a cell of benches, bins, hydrants, lamps or trunks by the bounding sphere round all its instances, so what stands near the camera is shaded wherever it is in the city), and they fade out over 0.25 s and are skipped while the camera moves faster than 8 m/s or turns faster than 1.6 rad/s, fading back in over 0.3 s once it slows. `StaticWorld.groundHeight` looks up decks and ramps in a 16 m grid (identical results to the old scan, about 5x faster). The player car's reflection cube (`src/render/localReflections.ts`, High only) leaves out street furniture with the moving things, but keeps the effects' flash and muzzle lights (`keepLights`): with them hidden, its faces had a different light set from the frame's, and every material in view built a second program on the first capture (about 30, in a frame of up to 3.9 s). The warm-up also filters a blank cube and compiles the player car's reflective finishes, and leaving the car releases the probe without disposing its filter, so the first drive of a session builds at most one program (the part batches' copy of the finished rims).

Motorbikes and boats are procedural, sculpted from lofts, sweeps and lathed parts (`src/render/shapeKit.ts`, `bikeModels.ts`, `hullGeometry.ts`, `boatModels.ts`); the CC0 bikes read as toys and the Kenney boats fit the specs badly, so none is imported. A bike draws its painted bodywork (the cars' paint program, a vertex tone per part), its frame and running gear and its steering parts (fork, bars, mudguard) itself, three draws; its lugged tyres, spoked or alloy rims and lamp lenses go through the part batches, and a coarse proxy on `SHADOW_LAYER` casts its shadow. Every non-paint part samples one shared palette material (a colour texture and a roughness/metalness texture, one texel per finish), so rubber, chrome, cast alloy and leather share a draw. A boat draws its painted topsides (a dinghy its tubes), one palette draw for the rest (vee bottom, cockpit, seats, console and wheel, rails, outboard), the speedboat's windscreen in the cars' glass, and a foam ring laid each frame on the drawn sea round its waterline (two sea samples per ring column; flat at the water elsewhere; absent when sinking or burnt). Its bow and stern lights go through the batches. Measured on High at 1920x1080 (all passes): a sport bike costs 7.8 calls and about 18k triangles, a scooter 7.8 and 12k, a speedboat 7.3 and 13k, a dinghy 6.3 and 13k (d98da7b: 30, 30, 17 and 14 calls). Bikes and boats fade in over `VEHICLE_FADE_IN` (0.5 s) where they appear, their riders with them (`RiderMount.level`), and fade out like cars. The Marina's moored cabin cruisers use the same hull builder (`mooredBoat`), inside the sim's hull and cabin boxes.

High's drawing buffer is capped at 3.5 million pixels (`HIGH_PIXEL_BUDGET` in `quality.ts`): a 1920x1080 window on a dpr-2 screen draws 2494x1403 instead of 2880x1620; at dpr 1 nothing changes. Contact shadows work at half resolution, draw their depth and normals only out to 110 m (fading out over the last 35 m) and take those draws from the frame's own opaque render list instead of walking the scene again (`shadesContact` picks them: an instanced batch such as a cell of benches, bins, hydrants, lamps or trunks by the bounding sphere round all its instances, so what stands near the camera is shaded wherever it is in the city), and they fade out over 0.25 s and are skipped while the camera moves faster than 8 m/s or turns faster than 1.6 rad/s, fading back in over 0.3 s once it slows. `StaticWorld.groundHeight` looks up decks and ramps in a 16 m grid (identical results to the old scan, about 5x faster). The player car's reflection cube leaves out street furniture with the moving things.

High adds local ambient occlusion before bloom and six nearby street lamps that light car bodies and facades at night. Lamp selection cross-fades, and broken lamps switch off immediately. Transparent effects and camera-dissolving foliage/characters are excluded from the contact-depth pass so invisible geometry cannot leave dark artifacts; sun and moon shadows still apply normally. Medium and Low keep the same resolution settings as before this visual pass.

### Fades and helicopter crashes

Characters on foot fade in over `PED_FADE_IN` (0.5 s) the first time they are drawn within 200 m, so peds spawning nearby no longer pop in; the player and car occupants are always drawn whole. The fade is a per-instance `instanceFade` attribute on the crowd's instanced parts, applied as an ordered (Bayer) dither in the existing character shaders, and the characters' `customDepthMaterial` applies the same dither so the shadow fades with the body. A character removed while drawn on foot (a cleared body or a despawned ped) keeps its last pose and fades out over `PED_FADE_OUT` (0.6 s). `crowd.firstDrawn` in the browser logs the last 400 first-drawn characters with their position and fade. Vehicles removed within `VEHICLE_FADE_RANGE` (150 m) of the camera and in view (cleared wrecks, despawned traffic) fade out over `VEHICLE_FADE_OUT` (0.6 s): `VehicleView.beginFade()` swaps in `alphaHash` copies of the materials of the meshes the car draws itself and gives its shadow body (and a far car's own dented or coarse body) an `alphaHash` depth material, keeping the version it was drawn in (a far car stays far); `endFade()` restores everything before the view returns to the pool. Its tyres, rims, lamps, roof gear and far body, glass and fittings stay in the shared part batches: every bucket carries a per-instance `instanceFade` with the same Bayer dither as the characters (in its own material copy, and in the far bodies' `customDepthMaterial`), so a fading car adds no draw calls (measured 0 per fading car on Medium and Low, slightly fewer than live on High, where fading bodies leave the contact pass). Warmup compiles the fade copies and the bucket programs, so a fade does not compile new shader programs mid-play. Software renderers pass `alphaHash` and the instance fade through to their simplified materials.

The part batches draw each bucket through a geometry of their own that shares the part's vertex buffers and adds the per-instance attributes. A bucket whose part material or geometry is disposed (the player's local-reflection rim copies, replaced on every finish change) is retired at the next frame's `begin()`, after the frame it was drawn in: its instanced mesh, geometry and material copy are freed, so transient materials do not accumulate. `GameRenderer.hookShadowMap()` wraps three's `shadowMap.render` to show `SHADOW_LAYER` to the shadow pass; three builds a new shadow map when a lost WebGL context is restored, so the hook is installed again on restore (and checked every frame), once per shadow map.

A crashing police helicopter trails fire and smoke every frame while it falls (`effects.heliCrash`). Its descent is tested against the solid colliders and the Ferris wheel's disc (`src/sim/heliCrash.ts`): it comes to rest on top of the first roof it meets (the whole height swept in a step is tested, so the longest 0.1 s frame cannot carry it through a 0.4 m garage slab), including the garages and the police station, with a smaller blast on a garage roof (`HELI_ROOF_BLAST`) so a car parked inside is not destroyed, and it stops against the lighthouse, crane legs and the Ferris wheel instead of falling through them.

## Assets and the asset library

Models, PBR textures and the sky HDRI live under `src/assets/` and are built from CC0 sources by `npm run assets` (`scripts/assets/README.md` describes the pipeline and the output conventions; `LICENSES-ASSETS.md` lists every source). `src/assets/manifest.json` has one entry per file: id, file, kind, byte size, bounds, license id, and for characters a bone map and for vehicles wheel sockets.

`src/render/assets/` loads them:

- **Preload.** `main.ts` waits for the fonts, then calls `assets.preload(onProgress, { backend: browserBackend })` behind the "LOADING SAN AZURA" screen, and builds the game only when it settles. The bar follows the bytes received (each file is streamed; progress is the received bytes over the manifest's byte total, never backwards, at least one update per asset). Requests use the content-hashed URLs Vite emits for `import.meta.glob(..., { query: '?url' })`, which are relative, so the build also works from a sub-path such as GitHub Pages `/gta7/`; `assetsInlineLimit` is 0 so no model becomes a `data:` URI.
- **Timeout and fallback.** Preload resolves, it never rejects or hangs: after `PRELOAD_TIMEOUT_MS` (12 s) it aborts what is still pending and settles. A file that fails (HTTP error, abort, corrupt data) or times out is missing from the library, logs a single `console.warn` (`GTA7 assets: <id> failed (...)`; Chrome adds its own network line), and its view builds the procedural model it always had. A manifest that cannot be read leaves the library empty (one warning). Responses that arrive after the library has settled are dropped, so a model never changes in the middle of play and no shader compiles because of it. `?assets=0` skips the preload.
- **Lookup.** Views never wait: `assets.model(id)`, `assets.clone(id)` (a copy sharing geometry and materials), `assets.texture(id)`, `assets.bounds(id)` and `assets.socket(id, name)` answer synchronously and return `undefined` for anything not loaded. `assets.use(id)` also reports an id the manifest does not know, once. Node tests see an empty library unless they call `loadAssetsFromDisk()` (`src/render/assets/testing.ts`), which loads the committed GLBs through the same GLTFLoader and meshopt decoder with placeholder textures.
- **Warm-up and context loss.** `GameRenderer.warmup()` adds `assets.warmGroup()` (one clone of every loaded model, parked below the map) to the scene while it compiles, so every asset material has its program before first use, on every tier and for the software tier's vertex-lit replacements too; the group is removed again straight after. The warm-up runs again after a tier switch and after a restored WebGL context. Textures keep their `ImageBitmap` (it is never closed), so three uploads them again after a context loss.
- **Debugging.** `window.__gta7.ext.assets` has `report()` (manifest size, loaded, failed ids, timeout flag, bytes, preload and decode milliseconds), `ids(kind?)`, `entry(id)`, `list()` and `warmGroup()`. The warm-up group is cached only once the preload has settled, so models that arrive after an early warm-up are warmed too.

Who uses what: seven car kinds the `veh.q.*` models (Vehicles), the buildings the ambientCG facade sets and Poly Haven's `box_profile_metal_sheet` (Buildings), the ground Poly Haven's `worn_asphalt`, `leafy_grass` and `coast_sand_01` (Ground), the trees the leaf atlas built from ambientCG's LeafSet024 (Props), and the lighting the Kloofendal sky HDRI (Lighting and sky). The people, street furniture, dressing, the motorbikes and boats, the procedural car kinds, the guns and the effects are modelled or generated in code; no asset was imported for them. `LICENSES-ASSETS.md` names every asset id under its source.

## Characters

People are chunky toy figures built in code (`src/render/characterGeometry.ts`): rounded segment parts (torso, hips, head with a face, hair and an optional sweep, caps, sunglasses, neck, deltoids, upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, shins and shoes, a collar, an untucked hem, and the police badge and duty belt) hinged on the game's own skeleton (`SKELETON` and the procedural pose in `src/sim/pose.ts`), with the feet planted by the gait (`src/render/gait.ts`). Each character's height, build, skin, shirt, trousers, hair and hat come from its sim appearance. `CrowdView` (`src/render/crowd.ts`) draws every body part as one `InstancedMesh` for the whole crowd, so a crowd costs one draw per part and pass whatever its size.
What is too small to model is painted per pixel (`src/render/characterPaint.ts`): from where a pixel falls on its part and the person's second colour and style (`instanceTrim`, `instanceStyle`, chosen by id in `dressOf`), the shader draws necklines, plackets, buttons, pockets, a jacket's lapels and the shirt under it, belts and buckles, the fly and the stitching of jeans, a cap's panels, cuffs, an officer's patches and the soles of shoes, with no texture, triangle or draw call; lines thinner than a pixel fade to their average.
Nobody further off is drawn cheaper: a person costs 3,548 triangles dressed plainly and 4,552 at most. Fades, the camera's lens dissolve and the foot contact shadows work the same on every tier and on the software renderer; `crowd.drawnHeads()` lists the drawn heads for tests.

## Buildings

Buildings draw with one shared facade program (`src/render/city/facadeShader.ts`): PBR stone, brick, plaster and metal from the WebP sets, an analytic box recess for every window cell with reveals that shade and self-shadow, relief sills, a rough/metal split for the glass, per-building tints, night windows lit per cell inside the recess, and a building fog that caps the haze on buildings so the towers keep their outline in the afternoon. Cornices (0.18 to 0.36 m) and parapets are geometry. Roofs carry HVAC units, water tanks, stair and lift housings, antennas, vents and roof gardens, merged into the cell batches (`rooftops.ts`). Four landmark towers (a stepped crown with a spire, a pyramid, an open crown and a wedge, `landmarks.ts`) are the tallest flat-roofed downtown and midtown towers chosen clear of each other along the sea sight lines. Shops have 178 frontage designs (`storefronts.ts`) with rooms behind the glass, signs, awnings with undersides and planters; at night about two thirds are lit at 22:30, and they close one by one until shortly after 02:00.
Plaster walls (`white_plaster_02`: stucco, houses, hotels and the curtain walls' spandrels) keep the set's fine grain but only `FacadeLook.stain` (0.2 to 0.3) of its broad light and shade: the shader divides the albedo by its mip 4 levels down (about 8 cm on the plaster) and puts that share back, one more texture fetch; at full strength the set's stains, tiled every 2.6 m, read as smudges on every house and apartment.
Every frontage cell with a door has its doorway (`DOOR_OPENING` in `textures/storefront.ts`) drawn 0.14 to 0.6 m behind the front, with reveals, a soffit and a threshold that take the cell's colour just outside the doorway (`frontQuad`); the revolving door's drum stays flush.
Piers of the building's trim (0.32 m wide, 0.1 m out) part the bays of a shop, lobby or loading band and clasp its corners, under a deeper cornice with a lip; parapets up to 45 m carry a coping in the trim's stone.
Hotels run a balcony along every floor of their street walls and 45% of the apartment blocks have one at every other window (`balconies`; of the special buildings only the Hotel Salgado, and on a setback tier none whose slab would meet the lower roof's parapet and coping); two brick blocks in three have a fire escape down a street wall at least three window columns wide, its drop ladder ending at least 2.4 m over the pavement (`fireEscape`); both are in the double-sided street decor.
String courses hang just under their floor lines, so a balcony's slab or a fire escape's landing on that line covers their tops; a landing stands `ESCAPE_FLOOR` (0.3 m) over its line, clear of the ground floor's cornice lip.
The bank's doorway floor is level with its top step (`DoorOpening.floor`, `BANK_STEP_TOP`), and a house's with its porch deck.
Trim under 10 cm (the gables' lap boards, shutters, plinths, copings, downpipes, the attic vents) is about 55,000 of the trim's 236,000 triangles city-wide and casts into the shadow maps with the rest.
As one shadowless mesh (one draw call more) it cost 30,000 to 240,000 more triangles a frame in the street views than it saved, because `CityView.add` splits only shadow casters into culled cells, so the whole batch was drawn in every pass; it stays in the trim.
The door floors take no headlight pool: they are in the storefront material, and the headlights are added to the ground's materials by the renderer.
City houses have lap-boarded gables in the wall's colour round an attic vent (the gable ends were the roof's dark material), barge and fascia boards, a ridge cap, a chimney on three in four, downpipes, a stone plinth with a sloped top (no level cap for the headlights' pools to light) and green shutters at the upper windows the facade paints (`houseShutters`); the county's farmhouses keep their plain roofs.
Each facade wall's per-vertex seed carries its tier's roof height in decimetres in its whole part and the building's night seed in thousandths in its fraction (`facadeSeed`; exact in a float32 below 409 m), so the shader knows where the cornice is.
The layout's heights are no whole number of storeys, and the top row of the window grid ran into the cornice on most buildings (seven brick blocks in ten): a window whose head comes within its style's headroom of the roof (`FACADE_TOP`, 0.35 to 1.2 m) is left out, and the band over the top floor becomes an attic; a house's top window is lowered (keeping 0.3 m over its floor) and shortened under the eaves if it stays 0.75 m tall (`facadeWindowFit`, which the shutters use too).
The upper floors are drawn in the shader, on every tier including Low's flat facades (`uDeco`): brick blocks get a stone lintel with a keystone or a gauged-brick arch over every window (by building), stone sills, a stone sill course under the top floor and a corbel table under the cornice; apartment blocks moulded hoods, a sill course and a painted frieze; offices piers between their bays (a ribbon, every other bay or every bay by building, every bay on the top floor) with reveals in the recess; a glass tier ending over 30 m has louvres in place of its top row of glass.
Downtown's three-tier glass towers draw their podium (10 to 16 m) in the office facade with a pier at every bay; the blue curtain walls have vision glass 2.3 m tall over a spandrel band, the teal ones a 0.3 m fin on every bay (`facadeRelief`).
Office and glass towers of eight storeys or more carry a plant penthouse (concrete round a louvred band, 4 to 5.5 m) clear of the layout's roof props, and three brick and apartment blocks in five raise the middle of their street parapet in two steps; together with the review fixes the buildings come to about 434,000 triangles city-wide (429,000 before), with no mesh or draw call added.
All of it rides in the existing trim, decor, roof and storefront batches, so the city's mesh count and programs are unchanged: the first pass took the buildings' triangles from about 265,000 to about 430,000 city-wide (about 5% more triangles in a street view with its shadows, draw calls the same).
`tests/unit/buildings_detail.test.ts` checks the stain share, the doorways, the normals, the mesh count, the balconies, the fire escapes, the shutters, the roofs in the facade seeds, the windows' fit under the roofs, the podiums and the penthouses.

`CityView.setQuality` sets the facade anisotropy (8x High, 4x Medium, 2x Low, 1 on software renderers) and the window parallax (High and Medium; Low and software renderers draw flat facades), uniforms only. The street decor and the stunt-ramp sides share the decor program (`DECOR_PROGRAM`), so a ramp first seen at night builds no program of its own.

## Time of day

The sky's image-based light (the baked environment map) is re-baked whenever the analytic sky colours change by about 2 % or the sun has fully set or risen, never less than half a real second after the previous bake, and at once after a jump in time (or every quarter game hour otherwise). Between bakes `scene.environmentIntensity` is scaled by the ratio of the sky's current to its baked irradiance (clamped to 0.5–2), so dawn and dusk brighten and darken every frame instead of in steps. This applies to every hardware tier with half-float targets; without them (and on software renderers) the sky light is an analytic hemisphere fill that was already continuous. The player car's local reflection probe recaptures after 0.05 game hours, still at most one capture start per half second. `__gta7.setClockRate(r)` and `__gta7.envStats()` help check this in the browser.

## Finish pass: colour grade and anti-aliasing

Every hardware tier ends its effects with `FinishPass` (`src/render/finishPass.ts`): High runs contact shadows, bloom, finish; Medium bloom, finish; Low finish alone. It works in linear HDR before three's own ACES and sRGB output, so tone mapping and colours stay the same pipeline on every tier. The grade (`gradeAt(hours, weather?)` in `src/render/grade.ts`) is keyframed over the day: the day (9:00 to 16:00) has fuller colour and a firmer curve (saturation 1.12, contrast 1.08), sunlit faces a little warm against shadows a little cool (it was all but neutral, and the city read as a model of itself), golden hour (18:24) is warmer, and night is cooler, slightly desaturated, with a stronger vignette. It applies a luminance-normalised white balance, saturation, contrast in log space around mid grey, a small split tone and a vignette; no parameter moves more than 1e-3 per game minute, midnight included. Cloud lowers saturation and contrast and rain deepens the vignette, from the sim's weather.

High and Medium keep 4x MSAA from the WebGL context. Low uses FXAA inside the same pass: three's `FXAAShader`, patched to detect edges and blend in a reversible tone-mapped proxy (`c / (1 + max(c))`), so edges against bright lamps are not biased toward the light and HDR values are not clamped. The module throws on load if the patch no longer matches three's shader. Software renderers and GPUs without half-float targets have an LDR output where three allows no effects, so they get neither FXAA nor the grade. Tier switches only change the pass's uniforms (no shader rebuild). `__gta7.setPost({ aa, grade, vignette })` overrides it until the next quality change, and `state().render.post` reports `{ aa, grade, vignette }`.

On the M4 Pro at 1920x1080 High the perf playtest stayed at 60 fps in all four segments (final frames 5.3–6.8 ms).

The pass also draws the rush of speed (`FinishPass.rush`, `rushAt(speed)`): in the player's car from 90 km/h, full from 205 km/h, the frame streaks towards the point the road runs to, not at all in the tall oval that holds the car and the street ahead and most at the edges.
It takes ten samples along each streak on High and five on Medium (ten cost the Radeon iGPU 3 fps), started at a per-pixel offset so their spacing shows as grain; there is none on Low, nor with Camera shake off.

What reaches the bloom is capped at `BLOOM_CEIL` (6, scene-linear, hue kept; `capBloom` patches `UnrealBloomPass`'s high-pass shader and throws if three's shader no longer has the line).
The sun's highlight on a car's clearcoat is a few pixels dozens of times brighter than anything else in the frame, and uncapped it bloomed into a white blob that hid the car.

## Chase camera

`chaseOffset` (`src/render/camera.ts`) is where the camera settles behind a vehicle, and `chaseFov` its lens: at rest 3.7 m plus 0.62 of the vehicle's length back and 1.0 m plus 0.58 of its height up, on a lens 10 degrees under the Field of view setting, so a car fills about a seventh of the frame's width (an eleventh on the 68 degree lens it had, from 5.2 m plus 0.72 of the length).
With speed the lens opens by up to 17 degrees while the boom closes in 14% and drops 20%, so the car keeps most of its size as the street stretches past it; the boom used to back away 18% as the lens widened, and its follow point trailed the car by its speed over 18 (2.8 m at 180 km/h), which together shrank the car by a third.
The follow point now aims that much ahead, so it keeps up with the car at any steady speed and still eases out a jolt.
The camera looks at the road 9 m ahead of the car (16 m at full speed), which puts the car in the frame's lower third and the horizon above the middle.
It has weight: it falls up to 0.8 m behind a car that pulls away and closes up to 0.55 m on one that brakes, for a change of pace only (it catches up with a car that keeps pulling), and it leans into a turn by up to 2.6 degrees (not with Camera shake off).

The motion check (`scripts/playtests/motion.mjs`, see the README) measured the car at 141 px of a 1600 px frame at rest and 115 px above 72 km/h before, and 242 px and 222 px now.

Round 8 on 2026-11-13 (`--scenario perf`, 1600x900, the machine otherwise idle): on the RTX 5090 at High every one of the 18 scenes at 60 fps, 205 to 1,009 draw calls, no console errors.
On the Radeon iGPU at Medium this build runs about 1 fps under `a746489`: the twelve scenes that are neither capped at 60 fps nor the one outlier (beach-dusk, 56 against 47) average 43.3 fps against 44.7, all 18 average 48.7 against 49.1, and the driving scenes were 1 to 3 fps lower in each of three runs (single runs, which differ by 2 to 3 fps a scene from one to the next): the new-game street 34 from 36, downtown 40 from 40, the four-star pursuit 41 from 41, the valley pass drive 39 from 41.
That is with Medium taking 80% of the fuller streets' traffic and 70% of their crowd, and five samples in the speed blur; at 90% of both the same three scenes ran at 33, 38 and 38.

## On-foot camera

On foot the camera stands `FOOT_BOOM` (3.2 m) back from the player's head along the view and `FOOT_SHOULDER` (0.42 m) out over his right shoulder, level with his head (`FOOT_LIFT`), on a lens 8 degrees under the Field of view setting (`footFov`, 60 degrees at the default 68).
He stands 47% of the frame's height there, his head just above the middle and left of the crosshair, his soles 8% clear of the bottom edge; it stood 4.3 m back on the full setting, 0.18 m higher, and showed him at 31%.
The far zoom (V) stands 4.6 m back on the full setting, about that old view (29%); aiming stands as it did, 2.4 m back, 0.62 m out and 0.18 m up (`FOOT_LIFT_AIM`) on a lens 16 degrees under the setting, his upper body at the frame's left, clear of the crosshair (level with his head, the crosshair met someone right beside his shoulder at head height and the gun shot him).
With his back to a wall the raised camera (see `RAISE_DEPTH`) opens the lens to the full setting as it rises (`RAISE_WIDE`): 1.2 m from a wall the foot lens held 63% of him in the frame, the setting's 72%.
On a setting under 67 degrees the boom stands further back (`footBoom`, 3.5 m on 62, 4.1 m on the narrowest, 55) so his feet stay in the frame; on the widest, 90, he is 33% of it.
The camera keeps up with him at a steady pace (its follow point aims ahead by its own easing; it trailed a sprinter by 0.34 m) and sprinting opens the lens by up to 4 degrees.
It has a little weight: a change of his pace is a change of the camera's own against his, which a critically damped spring (8.5 rad/s) takes out, so it drops about 0.18 m back as he sets off, drifts up to 0.32 m in as he stops from a sprint and swings a little wide as he turns off his line, and is back in place within half a second; none while aiming or swimming, and no bob or roll.
His pace is taken in the view's frame, so a pan of the view as he walks on (he turns with it) is the player's look and moves nothing; taken in the world, a quick pan at a sprint swung the camera out by its whole 0.32 m.
Leaving a car the follow point starts at his head and the boom eases in from as far back as the chase camera stood, as the aim's does; the follow point used to ease out of the car's body, which put the boom's root inside the car and the camera right over him looking a radian down for the first quarter second.
In the water the camera keeps the geometry it had on land before (4.3 m back, 6.6 m on the far zoom, 0.18 m up, the full setting's lens): it cannot dip under the surface, and the glide over quays and decks is tuned to it; on the closer boom a pan round a swimmer at a deck's edge turned the view 5.7 degrees in a frame.
An interaction marker nearer the lens than the player fades by the boom (`markerFade` in `src/render/markers.ts`, given `CameraController.markerView`): drawn in full while its axis lies within 0.1 m of as deep along the view as he is, gone 1.1 m nearer the lens, so the far zoom, the water and a narrow setting each move the band with their boom; on the 4.3 m boom those were 4.2 and 3.2 m from the lens, which on the 3.2 m boom left a marker a step from him half faded and one beside him at his own distance drawn whole and cut by the frame's edge.
The one he steps straight off fades by the lesser of his distance from it and the camera's from its axis (linear from 2 m down to 1.2 m): the camera reaches its beam as he is 2.1 m out, and multiplied smoothsteps took 0.066 of its opacity in one frame at a sprint.

## Night lights

`src/render/lightSprites.ts` draws the vehicles' night lights on every tier including software renderers. Lamp glows are camera-facing sprites at each working head, tail, brake, reverse and siren lamp (`VehicleView.lampWorldPositions()`), one draw call, so smashed lamps and destroyed cars leave no glow. Each glow's colour times intensity stays below the bloom threshold (2.6), so bloom does not flicker on them; brake glows are about 2.2 times the running tail glow. A second batch, light streaks, draws wet-road reflections (see Rain below). Lights turn on at night or in dark weather: `GameRenderer.weatherDim` follows `sim.weather.state.dim` every frame, and cars light up at `max(night, dim)` (rain and storm, and overcast; not clear or cloudy). `state().render` reports `glows`, `pools` (the cars whose headlights light the ground) and `streaks`.
Headlights light the ground in its own shading, as any light does, not in a decal over it.
`LightSprites` keeps eight lit cars on every tier (`HEADLIGHTS`): the player's own, when its lamps are lit, always, in the first slot and at its full strength, and the seven lit ones nearest the camera for the rest.
Every ground material (roads and their manholes and storm drains, lane paint, tyre marks, pavements, kerbs, lots, lawns, beaches, farm tracks, stunt ramps, bridges and decks: the materials of `WetGround` and `GroundSurfaces`; and the crop fields' slabs, `FieldsView.ground`, which are neither wet nor detailed, so the wheat keeps its ripples) is patched once (`lightGround`) to add, after all its other lights, its albedo times the headlights' irradiance to its direct diffuse light (`BRDF_Lambert( material.diffuseColor ) * POOL_LIGHT * shape * strength`).
So a car's headlights add the same light to a dark county road and to lamp-lit asphalt, the asphalt's grain, cracks and paint show under them as under any light, and wetness, fog and tone mapping treat it like the rest of the ground's light.
The crop fields' ground and plants (`FieldsView.ground` and `FieldsView.crops`: the slabs, and the corn, wheat and sunflower plants, whose world position carries each instance's matrix) take the same light by a rule of their own (`lightGround( materials, true )`): what the headlights add is held under `FIELD_LIGHT_MAX` (`fieldLight`, the shader's maths for tests: a luminance `y` becomes `y / sqrt( 1 + ( y / max )^2 )`, the colour scaled with it, so small light, up to 0.05, is kept within 3 per cent and pale straw's is not ten times the asphalt's), and no spot light shines on them (the player's is the only one within reach: a street lamp's spot reaches 22 m, and the nearest of the 1,149 lamps is 72 m from a field).
Both were needed for a field to read as lit ground at night.
Lit as asphalt is, the pale straw of the wheat slab (`#c9a95a`) took ten times the asphalt's light and read luma 202 to 211 at 7 to 20 m with the plants hidden and the pool alone lighting it, where the county asphalt reads 57 to 78, a flat yellow patch like a lamp's (on Low, with no spot, the wheat read 201 to 214).
And the spot, 160 cd falling with the square of the distance from a point 0.8 m up, lights an upright plant in front of the bumper straight on where it grazes the road: the wheat tufts 3 to 7 m ahead went to luma 235 to 255, past the bloom's threshold, a white blob with a halo (the owner's "glowing sun") that flashed as the tufts crossed the beam.
On a flat field at a steady 12 m/s the lit patch's mean luma moved by 2.2 between frames on average and 12.6 at most (a standard deviation of 5.3 over 5 s); with the spot off it moved by 0.1 and 1.0, with the plants hidden by 0.1 and 0.6, and with the ground's pool off it was unchanged, so the flash was the spot on the plants, not the pool, the car's pitch or the beam's chord (flat ground: the rise is 0 and the pool's strength 1 in every frame).
Now the wheat, plants and all, reads 120 to 138 at 7 to 20 m (154 to 235 before), as bright as lit pavement (130), the sunflowers' soil and plants 83 to 95 (before 94 to 122), lettuce 100 to 110 (121 to 137) and the furrows 52 to 63 (58 to 67), the asphalt's own 57 to 78; no pixel of the wheat reaches 145 (255 before) and the patch's mean luma moves by 0.07 between frames and 0.3 at most, and the wheat's ripples stay (they scale the slab's albedo, which the light takes).
The same fields at the same distances with the lamps off read 36 to 42 (wheat), 20 to 21 (soil), 25 (lettuce) and 19 (furrows).
The blocks that stand in for corn and sunflowers past the plants' ring keep the road's rule, spot included.
The patch is a fixed loop over uniform arrays shared by every ground material, so cars coming and going compile nothing and draw nothing; the others fade out over the last 5 m before the eighth's distance (or the 90 m range), so a car joining or leaving them does not pop. The player's car is outside that ranking because its bumper is about 11 m from the chase camera: ranked with the rest, a queue or a jam of nine lit cars about the camera took its beam down to 0.53 and 0.04 of its light.
The light's shape (`poolShape`, the shader's maths for tests) is one lobe per head lamp, widening with distance, so two show just ahead of the bumper and merge a few metres on.
It is a long beam, as a low beam is, and not a bright patch by the bumper: there is little light in the first metres (a quarter of its best at 5 m), it builds up to a broad hot zone 13 to 25 m out where it holds within a fifth of its best (a plateau of sorts, with its best at about 17 m down the middle), falls to under half of it by 35 m and to under a third at 40 m, and ends 50 m out (`POOL_LENGTH`).
From the chase camera the first 5 to 10 m of road lie just above the car's roof, and the beam runs out to the horizon from there.
The beam widens with distance, its half width 4.5 m at the bumper and 8.5 m at its far end, with soft sides.
It follows the ground's height under the bumper and its rise ahead, gives nothing to ground well off that plane (a bridge deck over the road, the foot of a quay) or facing away from the lamps (a kerb's far side, the road past a crest), and is warm (`POOL_LIGHT`).
On the county straight at 23:00 on High the player's car takes the road ahead from luma 19 to 54, 59, 62 and 77 at 5, 7, 10 and 13 m, 68 at 20 m, 41 at 35 m and 29 at 40 m (the shape before: 91, 82, 65, 64, 39, 19 and 19); downtown, where a street lamp already lights the road to 97 and 62 at 5 and 7 m, the same headlights take it to 121 and 104 (the multiplied pool before reached 223 and 204, near white, and an oncoming car's 173 against its street lamp's 104), and the dark stretch between two lamps, 18 with the headlights off, reads 68-78 (43-57 with the shape before).
The player's car gets the same light as every other: its headlight spot on High and Medium lights the cars, people and walls ahead (not the crop fields, above), and on the road adds about 16 luma at 5 m, 6 at 7 m and 3 at 10 m (the near light, with so little in the beam's first metres), mostly under the car's own body from the chase camera, so it is not discounted.

On the software tier's 8-bit canvas there is no ground shading to light and the road is nearly black, so there the headlights are a painted batch (`poolFragment`, `POOL_PAINT`), a colour added like the glows with the same shape, toned to about the hardware tiers' lit road (luma 52, 65 and 71 at 7, 10 and 13 m on the county straight); its decal is as wide as the beam at its far end, 17 m.

Street lamps have a plinth, a lighter metal tapered pole, a curved arm and a luminaire head with a lit lens. A knocked-down lamp hides every part, including its halo and pool.
A lamp's ground pool is its light laid on the ground, an additive decal 18 m across (`LAMP_POOL`) on every tier: the colour and cone of `LAMP_LIGHT` (`src/render/props.ts`), the same light as High's real lamp spots (`StreetLighting`), falling from 6.3 m up as the cube of the cosine off the vertical inside the cone's soft edge (`lampPoolShape`), so it is broad and flat-topped, 0.71 of its centre 3 m out and 0.22 at 6 m, with no hot spot under the lamp; at full night its centre adds 0.21 of the colour.

The lamp and Ferris wheel halos draw their glow in the shader, so there is no texture to upload when the lights come on at night.

The Ferris wheel's models are in `src/render/setPieceModels.ts`, and PropsView turns and places them.

Its two rims are braced into a truss by cross tubes and diagonals, 32 bays with a cross tube at each cabin's pivot, and 32 tension spokes run from the hub's flanges.
Its A-frames are octagonal tubes tapering to the hub, braced by a tie across each frame 15 m up and by struts and crossed diagonals beside the deck, all clear of the walkway and the cabins' sweep.
The wheel keeps its three meshes (rim, cabins, legs), so it adds no draw: from under it 3.3% more triangles on High, 5.5% on Medium and 3.1% on Low, the shadow pass included.

Port Basin's three cranes and the Azura Bowl are city geometry (`src/render/city/setPieces.ts`): one vertex-coloured geometry over a small atlas, in the city's cells, so each place is one draw where it is seen (the three cranes were three meshes of props.ts).
